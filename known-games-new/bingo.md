# Bingo — fill a row before anyone else

- From: Bingo, a number game played in church halls and clubs across North America (known as Tombola in Italy)
- Players: 3 or more players
- Time: 15 min
- Made by: Traditional; Italian in origin, the book says
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 113
- This version: A 5 by 5 card on paper, numbers 1 to 100, and a caller

A caller draws numbers from a bowl one at a time and reads them out. Players mark the numbers on their own cards. The first to fill a row shouts Bingo.

## What it shows a designer

Every player has a different card, but all of them are fed by the same caller. It shows how a game can be nearly all luck and still keep a room watching, because every number called might be the one you need.

## What you need

- scraps of paper numbered 1 to 100 in a bowl or hat
- a sheet of paper and a pencil for each player
- about 20 small markers for each player, such as buttons or coins

## How to play

1. **Make the cards.** Each player draws a 5 by 5 grid and writes B I N G O across the top. Fill column B with numbers from 1 to 20, I with 21 to 40, N with 41 to 60, G with 61 to 80 and O with 81 to 100. No number twice.
2. **Choose a caller.** One player is the caller for this game. The caller shakes the bowl.
3. **Call.** The caller draws one scrap and reads the number aloud. Anyone with that number on their card puts a marker on it.
4. **Keep going.** The caller keeps drawing and reading numbers until a player has filled a row.
5. **Win.** The first player to cover a whole row, a whole column or a whole diagonal shouts Bingo. The caller checks the card against the called numbers.

## After playing, talk about

- How much did your own choices matter after you wrote your card?
- What would you change so players could affect each other?
- Why do players keep watching when the game is all chance?

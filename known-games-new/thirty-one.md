# Thirty-One — gather one suit, then knock

- From: Thirty-One, an old card game (also called Scat)
- Players: 2–7 players, best with 4 or more
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Thirty-One'
- This version: One deal per round with no banking version and no extra hands for three of a kind

Each player holds three cards and tries to get their total in one suit as close to 31 as possible. On your turn you swap one card, or you knock to end the round. The player with the lowest hand pays a chip, and players leave the game when their chips are gone.

## What it shows a designer

Every turn is a small choice: take what everyone can see, or take an unknown card. Knocking turns a private guess about your hand into a public risk. A chip that leaves the table each round slowly removes players.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards
- about 3 coins or buttons for each player
- a pot space in the middle for lost chips

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Everyone starts with 3 chips. Aces count 11, face cards count 10, other cards count their number. Deal 3 cards to each player. Put the rest face down as the stock and turn one card face up beside it.
2. **Take and drop.** On your turn, take the top card of the stock or the face-up card. Then drop one card from your hand face up. You always hold three cards.
3. **Count your hand.** Only cards of one suit are added together. Your score is your best single suit. Ace, king and 10 of one suit make 31 and end the round at once.
4. **Knock.** Instead of taking a card, you may knock on the table if you think your total beats the others. Everyone else then gets one last turn.
5. **Pay.** All hands are shown. The lowest hand pays one chip into the pot. If several hands tie for lowest, each of them pays one.
6. **Run out.** When your chips are gone, you may play on until you next have the lowest hand. Then you are out. The last player left takes the pot.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you knock, and what did you know about the other hands?
- Why did you take a card from the table instead of the stock?
- How did losing chips change how brave players were?

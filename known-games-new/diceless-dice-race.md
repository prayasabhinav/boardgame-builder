# Diceless Dice Race — choose your roll from a list

- From: Diceless dice games, a rule change for any game that uses two dice
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: R. Wayne Schmittberger, who suggests it for backgammon, Monopoly and other dice games, and for games played by post
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 224
- This version: A 60-square paper race, and landing on the other player's square sends them back to the start

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players race along a track. Nobody throws a dice. Each player has a list of all 36 two-dice rolls and picks one from it each turn, crossing it off. Once all 36 are used, the list starts again.

## What it shows a designer

Take the dice out of a dice game and the game stops being about luck. It shows what happens when the odds are replaced by a list both players can see: every choice has a cost, because a roll used now is gone later.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a track of 60 squares drawn in a line, numbered 0 to 60
- a second sheet for each player with all 36 rolls written out: 1-1, 2-1, 3-1, 4-1, 5-1, 6-1, then 1-2, 2-2, and so on up to 6-6
- two small counters, one for each player, such as coins
- a pencil each

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Put both counters on square 0. Each player has their own sheet with all 36 rolls on it. Both players can look at each other's sheet at any time.
2. **Choose a roll.** On your turn, pick any roll on your sheet that is not crossed off. Cross it off.
3. **Move.** Add the two numbers together and move your counter forward that many squares. A roll of 3-5 moves you 8.
4. **Bump.** If you land on the square where the other counter stands, their counter goes back to square 0.
5. **Last roll.** When you cross off your 36th roll, you start again with a fresh sheet. On your last roll before that you have no choice, because only one is left.
6. **Win.** The first counter to reach square 60 or beyond wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which rolls did you save for later, and which did you use at once?
- Could you work out what the other player could do next? How did that change your choice?
- What did the game lose when the dice went, and what did it gain?

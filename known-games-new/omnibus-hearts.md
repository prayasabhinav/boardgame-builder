# Omnibus Hearts — one card to catch, and a chance to take it all

- From: Hearts, a card game; Omnibus Hearts is a form of Black Lady
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Omnibus Hearts'
- This version: Four deals, and the player with the lowest total after the fourth deal wins

Play it as Black Lady, with one new card that is good to win. The Jack of Diamonds counts plus 10 for whoever takes it. A player who takes all the hearts, the Queen of Spades and the Jack of Diamonds scores a big gain.

## What it shows a designer

In most Hearts games every special card is a danger. Here one card pulls the other way, so a player has to decide whether to chase it. It shows how a single reward in a game of avoiding can change how a player reads their own hand.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Deal and pass.** The dealer gives out the whole pack, thirteen cards each. Each player passes three cards face down to the player on their left before looking at the cards they receive.
2. **Lead and follow.** The player to the dealer's left leads any card. Everyone must follow suit if they can. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, and there are no trumps. The winner leads next.
3. **Score the cards you take.** Each heart counts 1 point against you. The Queen of Spades counts 13 against you. The Jack of Diamonds counts 10 for you, so subtract it from your total.
4. **Take all.** A player who wins all thirteen hearts, the Queen of Spades and the Jack of Diamonds scores a gain of 26 instead of a loss of 16.
5. **Play four deals.** Pass the deal to the left. The lowest total after four deals wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Did you chase the Jack of Diamonds even if it meant taking hearts?
- How did other players stop someone who tried to take it all?
- What does a reward card change about a game of avoiding?

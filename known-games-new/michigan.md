# Michigan — bid for the spare hand, then play the stops

- From: Michigan (also Newmarket or Boodle), a traditional card game
- Players: 3 to about 8 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; played for buttons or counters at parties
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Michigan'
- This version: One deck. Four paper slips stand for the layout cards (ace of hearts, king of spades, queen of diamonds, jack of clubs), and the matching cards in the deck are the boodle cards.

Each player starts with equal counters. All the cards are dealt, with one extra hand called the widow. The dealer may keep, swap or sell the widow. Players then play a rising run of cards in one suit until it stops. Matching a boodle card wins the counters piled on it.

## What it shows a designer

An auction for a hand nobody has seen, followed by a play where each run stops when the next card is missing. It shows how an auction and a hand game can sit together, and how counters left on a card build up until someone wins them.

## What you need

- one deck of 52 playing cards
- about 20 counters each, such as coins or buttons
- four slips of paper, one for each boodle card

## How to play

1. **Set the layout.** Put the four slips in a square. Before each deal, the dealer puts 2 counters on each slip and every other player puts 1.
2. **Deal.** Deal every card one at a time, with one extra hand more than there are players. Put the extra hand (the widow) to the dealer's left.
3. **The auction.** The dealer looks at their hand. The dealer may swap it for the widow, or keep it and offer the widow for sale. Others bid counters, and the highest bidder gets it, paying the dealer. If nobody bids, set the widow aside.
4. **Play a run.** The player left of the dealer leads their lowest card in any suit. Whoever holds the next higher card of that suit plays it, and so on. Say each card aloud.
5. **When a run stops.** It stops at the ace or when the next card is missing. The last player starts a new run in a different suit, with their lowest card in it. If they have only the old suit, they pass.
6. **Boodle cards.** If you play a card that matches a slip, take the counters on that slip. Uncollected counters stay for the next deal.
7. **Go out.** The first player to empty their hand wins. Every other player pays the winner one counter for each card left.

## After playing, talk about

- Was it worth paying for the widow? What did you know when you bid?
- Which runs did you stop on purpose, and how did you know where they would stop?
- What happened to the counters left on the slips?

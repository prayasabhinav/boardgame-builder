# Screw Your Neighbor — empty your pile before they empty theirs

- From: Screw Your Neighbor (also called Cat and Mouse), a card game for two
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; reworked in the late 19th century from the Continental game Crapette
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Screw Your Neighbor'
- This version: Pay-off piles of 12 cards, and no jokers

Each player has a pile of cards to get rid of, and both build ascending stacks in the middle from the same cards. Each turn you play what you can, then put one card on a private side stack to end your turn.

## What it shows a designer

Everything you put on the middle stacks helps your opponent too. It shows how a shared space turns a race into a game of timing: what you play now, what you hold back and where you choose to stop.

## What you need

- two packs of 52 playing cards with different backs
- a flat table with room for four middle stacks and eight side stacks

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Take 12 cards from one pack for each player. These are the pay-off piles, face down. Turn the top card of each pile up. The higher card (king high, ace low) goes first.
2. **Hands.** Shuffle the other pack. Deal five cards to each player and put the rest face down as the stock.
3. **Draw.** At the start of your turn, draw from the stock until you hold five cards.
4. **Play to the middle.** Any ace from your hand, your pay-off pile or a side stack must go to the middle at once, starting a stack. There can be four middle stacks. Build each stack up in order, any two on an ace, any three on a two, and so on. Both players may play to any middle stack. When a stack reaches king, shuffle it into the stock.
5. **Side stacks.** You may have four side stacks. Only you can play to them, and only from your hand. Build them down in order or with matching ranks. Putting a card on a side stack ends your turn. You may also end your turn by saying so.
6. **Win.** The pay-off pile top card may only go to the middle, and then you turn up the next one. The first player to empty their pay-off pile wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you hold a card back because playing it helped your opponent?
- How did a full set of side stacks change what you could do?
- Is a game where both players use the same stacks a race or a fight?

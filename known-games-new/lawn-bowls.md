# Lawn Bowls — land closest to the jack

- From: Lawn Bowls, an old lawn game for two sides
- Players: 2–8 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 240
- This version: Coins slid along a long table or smooth floor, in place of bowls on grass, and a game to 10 points instead of 50

Two sides take turns sliding coins towards a small marker, called the jack. When everyone has played, the side with the coin nearest the jack scores a point for each of its coins that is nearer than the other side's best.

## What it shows a designer

A score can come from distance alone. It shows how only the better of two positions matters, so the one coin that lies nearest the target can cancel several coins from the other side.

## What you need

- a long table or a smooth floor
- a button or small coin of a different kind, for the jack
- four coins for each side, with a different look for each side (for example 1-rupee coins and 2-rupee coins)
- a pencil and paper for scores

## How to play

1. **Pick sides.** Form two sides. Each side has its own kind of coin. A side may be one player.
2. **Throw the jack.** A player from one side slides the jack along the table, a good distance away. It stays where it stops.
3. **Slide.** Players from the two sides take turns standing at the same starting point and sliding a coin so it stops as near the jack as possible.
4. **Score the end.** When all the coins have been slid, look at which side has a coin nearest the jack. That side scores one point for each of its coins that lies nearer the jack than the other side's nearest coin.
5. **Play again.** Clear the table, start another end, and keep a running score.
6. **Win.** The first side to reach 10 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which coins mattered in the score, and which did not count at all?
- How did you decide how hard to slide?
- What would change if a coin could knock the jack?

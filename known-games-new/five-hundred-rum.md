# Five Hundred Rum — score points for every set you lay down

- From: Five Hundred Rum, also called Michigan Rum
- Players: 2–8 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in the United States
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Five Hundred Rum'
- This version: One pack of 52 cards and play to 200 points instead of 500

You lay sets on the table as in Rummy, but now each card you lay down scores points for you, and each card left in your hand counts against you. Going out first earns nothing extra.

## What it shows a designer

Points come from what you put down, not from finishing first. It shows how a scoring rule can make players hold back, pick up a pile of discards, or lay everything down at the last safe moment.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- a pen and paper to keep score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal 13 cards each with two players, 7 each with three or four. Put the rest face down as the stock and turn the top card face up beside it to start the discard pile.
2. **Draw.** Take the top card of the stock or the top card of the discards. You may also take two or more cards from the top of the discard pile, even the whole pile, but only if you lay down the lowest card you took in a set that same turn.
3. **Meld and lay off.** Lay down groups (three or four of one rank) or runs (three or more of one suit in order; ace may be high or low). You may add cards to anyone's sets. Keep any cards you add in front of you, because they score for you.
4. **Discard.** End your turn by discarding one card. You need not discard if you laid down your last card.
5. **End.** The deal ends when a player has no cards left. If the stock runs out, play on only while each player in turn takes from the discard pile.
6. **Score.** Face cards count 10, other cards the number on them. An ace counts 15, but 1 in A-2-3. Add up the cards you laid down. Subtract the cards left in your hand, aces at 15. The result, plus or minus, goes on your running total.
7. **Win.** The first player to reach 200 points wins. If two players pass 200 in the same deal, the higher total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you wait to lay down your sets, and what made you stop waiting?
- Did you throw away a card to make a pile worth taking later?
- How did scoring for laid-down cards, with no prize for going out, change your choices?

# Domineering — one lays upright, one lays flat

- From: Domineering, a pencil-and-paper game from mathematics
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 5–10 min
- Made by: Traditional; described in the mathematics of games
- Rules from: Characteristics of Games, p. 267
- This version: A 6×6 grid on paper, drawn with a pen each in two colours

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players place dominoes, each covering two squares, on a grid. One player may only lay dominoes upright and the other only flat. Whoever cannot place one on their turn loses.

## What it shows a designer

The two players have the same goal but not the same moves. It shows how giving each side a different rule changes which spaces matter to whom, and how a gap that is useless to one player can be worth a move to the other.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a grid of 6 squares by 6 squares
- two pens of different colours

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Draw 6 squares across and 6 squares down. Choose who is Upright and who is Flat.
2. **Place.** On your turn, shade two squares that touch, with both squares empty. Upright shades two squares one above the other. Flat shades two squares side by side.
3. **Keep going.** Take turns. Shaded squares stay shaded for the rest of the game.
4. **Lose.** If you cannot place a domino of your own kind on your turn, you lose.

## After playing, talk about

- Which empty gaps could only you use, and which could only your rival use?
- When did you take a space just to stop your rival using it?
- How would play change on a board with an odd number of squares?

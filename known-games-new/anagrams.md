# Anagrams — steal words from the table

- From: Anagrams, a word game played with letter tiles
- Players: 3–4 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: Traditional word game; the book says it was most popular several decades ago
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 83
- This version: Letters are written on small paper slips instead of tiles

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Letters are turned over one at a time. Anyone who sees a word calls it out and takes the letters. Other players can steal a word by adding letters to it and making a new word.

## What it shows a designer

Nobody waits for a turn, so quick eyes decide who gets words. It also shows how a rule that lets players take from each other keeps every word at risk.

## What you need

- about 100 paper slips, each with one letter written on it (more common letters should be written more often, such as many E, A and T and few Q or Z)
- a dictionary, or an agreement on which words count

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Mix the slips and put them face down on the table in a pile.
2. **Turn letters.** One player turns a slip face up. Then the next player turns one, and so on round the table. Everyone watches the faceup letters.
3. **Call a word.** Anyone who sees a word that can be made from the faceup letters calls it out. They take those slips and set them in front of themselves in the right order. They go back to turning letters.
4. **Steal.** A player can steal a word from in front of someone else. To do this, they add at least one faceup letter to all the letters of that word and call out a new word. They take the letters and the old word and put the new word in front of themselves.
5. **Not a steal.** Adding only an ending such as -S or -ER, or a start such as RE-, is not enough. The root word has to change.
6. **Your own word.** You may steal your own word to make it longer and harder for others to steal.
7. **Which words count.** Agree before play. Usually any word in the chosen dictionary counts, except names, short forms and words with hyphens.
8. **End.** The game ends when no slips are left in the pile, or when no one can use the ones left. The player with the most slips in front of them wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you steal a word, and when did you wait for a longer one?
- Which was harder to hold: a short word or a long one?
- How did agreeing on which words count change the game?

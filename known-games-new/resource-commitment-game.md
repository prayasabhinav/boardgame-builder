# Resource commitment game — share out ten points in secret

- From: A model game in Characteristics of Games, found inside many war and strategy games
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 5–10 min
- Made by: Not named in the book; a standard model from the study of games, and the book notes that Diplomacy fights at a tactical level work much this way
- Rules from: Characteristics of Games, p. 104
- This version: The book gives one round only; here the pair plays five rounds, and a column where both write the same number counts for nobody

Each player has ten points to put into three buckets, A, B and C, without the other seeing. Both reveal at the same moment, and whoever wins two of the three buckets wins the round.

## What it shows a designer

Anything that lets you share a fixed amount between places hides a small guessing game. If it always pays to put everything in one place, the guessing vanishes, so the designer has to make spreading out worth it.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper for each player
- a pen or pencil for each player
- a way to cover your sheet, such as a book or your hand

## How to play

1. **Draw the buckets.** Each player writes A, B and C across the top of their own sheet.
2. **Share out ten.** In secret, write a whole number under each letter. The three numbers must add up to ten. A bucket may hold zero.
3. **Reveal together.** Count three, then turn both sheets over at the same moment.
4. **Compare bucket by bucket.** The higher number wins that bucket. If both wrote the same number, nobody wins it.
5. **Score the round.** Whoever wins two or more buckets wins the round. If neither does, nobody scores.
6. **Play five rounds.** Keep a tally on a separate sheet. The player with more rounds after five wins. Swap what you try each round.

## After playing, talk about

- Did you ever put all ten into one bucket, and what made you do it?
- How did you try to guess where the other player would put their points?
- What would change if the rules gave each player twenty points and five buckets?

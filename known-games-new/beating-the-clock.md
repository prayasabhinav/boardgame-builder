# Beating the Clock — bid how fast you can act it out

- From: Charades, the old party game, with bidding added
- Players: 4 or more players in two or more teams
- Time: 20 min
- Made by: Traditional party game; the bidding version is described by Robert Schmittberger
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 148
- This version: Phrases are tabloid headlines or fortune-cookie sayings, as the book suggests; players are told which kind it is

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

One player from each team sees a phrase and bids how quickly they could get their team to say it, by acting it out in silence. The lowest bidder acts. If the team guesses within the bid, it earns a point.

## What it shows a designer

Here bidding is paid in time instead of money. It shows how an auction can be added to any game that has a clock, and how a bid is a promise that the bidder has to keep.

## What you need

- a phone or stopwatch
- slips of paper with phrases: tabloid headlines or fortune-cookie sayings
- paper and pencil for the score

## How to play

1. **Show the phrase.** One player from each team looks at the next phrase. Nobody else looks.
2. **Bid.** Bidders take turns bidding a time, such as two minutes. Each new bid must be lower than the last by at least 10 or 15 seconds.
3. **Stop.** When no one will bid lower, the lowest bidder becomes the actor for their team.
4. **Act.** Start the clock. The actor acts out the phrase, and teammates try to name it.
5. **Score.** If a teammate says the phrase within the bid, the team scores a point. If not, the team loses a point. Play until each team has had several turns; the highest score wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What made you bid lower than you felt sure about?
- How did seeing the same phrase as the other bidder change your bid?
- What would you add to make the bidding riskier?

## Its blocks

- Core mechanic: bidding; playing in silence; calling a contract

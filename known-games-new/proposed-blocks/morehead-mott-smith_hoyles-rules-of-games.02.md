## Proposed block: melding
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: melding
- Why no block covers it: Set collection is about gathering matching cards, and it misses laying finished sets face up on the table and adding cards to them, yours or other players'.
- Games in this book that use it: Basic Rummy (under 'Basic Rummy'), Contract Rummy, Five Hundred Rum, Canasta and Coon-Can (under their own headings), Panguingue (under 'Panguingue / Pan'). Knock Rummy and Gin Rummy keep sets hidden until the end.
- ? text: You lay a finished set of cards face up on the table. From then on you, and sometimes other players, can add matching cards to it.

## Proposed block: taking the discard pile
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: taking-the-pile
- Why no block covers it: Hand management is about choosing which of your own cards to play, and it misses taking a whole pile of other players' thrown-away cards into your hand.
- Games in this book that use it: Canasta and its many forms (under 'Canasta'), where you take the whole pile if you can use its top card; Five Hundred Rum, where you may take a batch from the pile if you use its lowest card (under 'Five Hundred Rum'); Oklahoma or Arlington (under 'Canasta').
- ? text: Every card players throw away goes in one pile. On your turn you may pick up the whole pile, as long as you can use the card on top straight away.

## Proposed block: betting on your hand
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: betting-on-your-hand
- Why no block covers it: Bidding is offering money to buy a prize, and it misses putting chips in a pot for the right to keep a hand in play, with the choice to call, raise or drop out.
- Games in this book that use it: Draw Poker, Stud Poker, Dealer's Choice games such as Anaconda, Cincinnati and Knock Poker, and Texas Hold'em (all in the Poker section). Canasta and Rummy games use no pot.
- ? text: Players put chips in a pot while they still think their hidden cards can win. You can match a bet, raise it, or drop out and lose what you put in.

## Proposed block: shared cards in the middle
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: shared-cards
- Why no block covers it: Hand management is about cards only you hold, and it misses cards turned face up in the middle that every player may use in their own hand.
- Games in this book that use it: Texas Hold'em (under 'Texas Hold'em'), Cincinnati, Criss-Cross, Lamebrain Pete and Omaha (under their own headings in Dealer's Choice), Spit in the Ocean (under 'Spit in the Ocean').
- ? text: Some cards are turned face up in the middle, and everyone can use them. You mix them with the cards in your own hand to make the best set you can.

## Proposed block: wild cards
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: wild-cards
- Why no block covers it: Set collection counts cards of one kind, and it misses cards that can stand for any other card the holder chooses.
- Games in this book that use it: Poker with jokers or deuces wild (under 'Wild-Cards' and 'Deuces Wild'), Baseball and Football (under their headings), Contract Rummy, Continental Rummy and Canasta, where jokers and twos stand for any rank.
- ? text: Some cards can stand for any other card you choose. A wild card can finish a set that would otherwise be missing one piece.

## Proposed block: pie rule
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: pie-rule
- Why no block covers it: bidding (nearest) is an offer for a prize, but this rule has one player set up a start or a first move and the other player choose which side to take.
- Games in this book that use it: Tablut, where one player makes a move for each army and names a bid, and the other picks a side (pp. 25–26); Hex, where one player places the first stone and the other picks a side (pp. 26–27); Renju, with a longer version of the same idea (p. 27); Double Unbalanced Chess (p. 36).
- ? text: One player sets up the start of the game, and then the other player picks which side to play. So the first player tries to make both sides even, like cutting a pie when someone else chooses the slice.

## Proposed block: two games at once
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: two-games-at-once
- Why no block covers it: take turns (nearest) says who acts when, but not that players run two or more boards side by side, each holding one side on one board and the other side on the other.
- Games in this book that use it: Duplicate Backgammon, where both players use each roll on two boards (p. 32); Double Tablut, with a move on each board every turn (pp. 33–35); Double Unbalanced Chess (pp. 35–37); Fox and Geese played twice at once (p. 35); Chess-Checkers-Chinese Checkers, where each turn you pick one board (p. 39); Backgammon-Parcheesi-Monopoly (p. 40).
- ? text: You play two games at the same time on two boards. You are one side on the first board and the other side on the second, so you must attack and defend at once.

## Proposed block: wraparound board
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: wraparound-board
- Why no block covers it: tile placement (nearest) builds a map by laying tiles, but does not cover a board whose edges are joined, so a play leaving one edge comes back in on the opposite edge.
- Games in this book that use it: Toric Scrabble, where words run off one edge and continue on the opposite one (pp. 17–18); Spherical Scrabble, where words cross a pole (pp. 18–19); Cylindrical Go and Toric Go (p. 62).
- ? text: The edges of the board are joined. If your line runs off the right side it comes back in on the left, like a video game character walking out of one side of the screen and into the other.

## Proposed block: capture by surrounding
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: capture-by-surrounding
- Why no block covers it: area control (nearest) counts who holds spots, and jumping over pieces (proposed from another book) removes a piece by hopping; neither covers taking a piece by placing your own pieces around it.
- Games in this book that use it: Tablut and Hnefatafl, where a piece is taken when two rivals sandwich it on a line (pp. 23–24, 29); Pente and Keryo Pente, where a pair or a line of three is sandwiched (p. 52); Go, where a stone or group with no empty point next to it is removed (pp. 58–59).
- ? text: You do not hop or hit a piece. You take it by placing your pieces on both sides of it, or all around it, so it has no room left.

- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: capture-by-surrounding
- Why no block covers it: Already proposed in the file schmittberger_new-rules-for-classic-games.01.md; this is an addition to that proposal, not a second block. Area control counts who holds spots and misses taking a piece by closing in on it.
- Games in this book that use it, with pages: Go and its variants One-Capture Go and Omino Go, where a group with no empty point next to it is taken (pp. 63–65); Reversi, where a line of pieces between two of yours is taken (p. 94); Ming-Mang, with a sandwich along a row or column (p. 96); Hexade, where a pair of stones is sandwiched (p. 116); Lotus and Medusa, where a group with no empty point next to it is taken (pp. 120, 123).
- ? text: As already proposed: you take a piece by placing yours on both sides of it, or all round it, so it has no room left.

## Proposed block: connecting across the board
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: connection
- Why no block covers it: Area control (nearest) counts who holds spots, and pattern building matches shapes; neither covers winning by joining two sides, edges or corners of the board with an unbroken chain of your own pieces.
- Games in this book that use it, with pages: Havannah, where a chain joining three edges (a fork) or two corners (a bridge) wins, as does a ring (p. 116); Hex, named as the same kind of connection game (p. 116); Hexade, where a line of six is one of the three winning shapes (pp. 115–116).
- ? text: You win by linking up, not by taking pieces. Your stones have to make one unbroken chain from one side of the board to another, and the other player tries to cut it.

## Proposed block: a shape that cannot be captured
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: safe-shape
- Why no block covers it: Area control (nearest) is about holding spots, and the nearest proposed block, capture by surrounding, is about taking pieces; neither covers a special shape that makes your pieces immune to capture.
- Games in this book that use it, with pages: Rosette, where a group containing a ring of six stones around a hexagon is safe (p. 66); Lotus, where a group containing one of the seven six-point rings can never be captured (pp. 119, 121); Medusa, where a ring makes a group safe only while it stays unbroken (p. 125).
- ? text: Some shapes are safe. If your stones make the shape, nothing around them can take them. It gives you a goal to build towards while the other player tries to surround you.

## Proposed block: chance alone decides
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: chance-alone-decides
- Why no block covers it: Push your luck still asks the player to decide when to stop, and this misses games where the throw or the card turned over settles everything and nobody chooses.
- Games in this book that use it, with pages: Classic Dice, where three dice are thrown three times and added (p. 181); Raffles, Multiplication and Going to Boston, where the dice give the total (p. 182); Ace in the Pot, where a throw of one or six moves a chip (p. 183); Help Your Neighbour, where a thrown number is crossed off (p. 184); War, where the higher turned-up card wins (p. 169).
- ? text: The dice or the cards decide everything. You throw or turn a card, and the result says who gains. There is nothing to choose, so the game is a short wait for luck.

## Proposed block: shared pot
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: shared-pot
- Why no block covers it: Bidding is offering money for one item, and this misses a pot everyone pays into at the start, which one winner takes and which stays and grows when a round has no winner.
- Games in this book that use it, with pages: Speculation, where the holder of the highest trump takes the pot (p. 161); Spoil Five, where each spoiled hand adds more to the pool (p. 163); Twenty-One, where stakes go into a pool (p. 166); Domino Loo, where each trick takes a fifth of the pool and a looed player pays into the next one (p. 191); Domino Pool (p. 191); Ace in the Pot, where chips fall into a pot until one player holds the last chip (p. 183).
- ? text: Everyone puts a counter into the middle before play. One player wins it all. If nobody wins, it stays there and grows, so the next round is worth more.

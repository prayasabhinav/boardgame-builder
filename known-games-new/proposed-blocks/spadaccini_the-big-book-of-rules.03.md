## Proposed block: finish on an exact number
- Slot: victory-condition
- Id: finish-on-exact-number
- Why no block covers it: Race to finish names the first to reach the end, and it misses games where passing the target is as bad as falling short, so the last step has to land exactly.
- Games in this book that use it: Twenty-One, a basketball game where a player at 20 points misses on purpose (p. 227); Darts, where a score must reach exactly zero on a double (p. 242); Cribbage, where the running count must stop at 31 and landing on exactly 31 earns extra (p. 284); Blackjack, where going over 21 loses (p. 270).
- ? text: You cannot just get there first. You have to land exactly on the target number, and going past it costs you the win or the hand.

## Proposed block: earn another turn
- Slot: timing
- Id: earn-another-turn
- Why no block covers it: Take turns says one player acts at a time in order, and it misses rules where doing well keeps the turn with you.
- Games in this book that use it: Croquet, where passing a wicket or hitting another ball earns extra strokes (p. 238); Twenty-One, where a made foul shot lets the shooter go again (p. 227); Badminton, where the server keeps serving after winning a point (p. 218); Bocce, where the side that scores starts the next round (p. 231).
- ? text: When you do something well, you keep the turn or get another one. Players who start well can play many moves while others wait.

## Proposed block: chase and tag
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: chase-and-tag
- Why no block covers it: Dexterity (nearest) is about steady hands and aim, and it misses games where one player chases the others and touching one of them hands over the chaser's role.
- Games in this book that use it: Tag and its variations, including Secret Tag, Stoop Tag, Iron Tag and Japanese Tag (pp. 257–258); Hide-and-Seek, where the finder chases a hider who tries to run home (p. 251); Duck Duck Goose (p. 276); Marco Polo (p. 277); Mother May I, where the aim is to tag the Mother (p. 277); Red Light, Green Light (p. 279).
- ? text: One player is It and chases the rest. When It touches someone, that person becomes It, and the chase starts again.

## Proposed block: relay
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: relay
- Why no block covers it: Roll and move sends one piece along a path, and it misses a team race in which each member does a short leg and hands over to the next.
- Games in this book that use it: Tag the Wall Relay and Relay Flag Races (pp. 256–257), Medicine Ball with its Arch Ball and Under and Over variations (pp. 243–244), Crisscross (p. 236), Preliminary Ball (p. 234), Pass Ball (p. 234).
- ? text: Your team runs a race in legs. One player goes, hands over a ball or a flag to the next, and the first team to finish every leg wins.

## Proposed block: hide and find
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: hide-and-find
- Why no block covers it: Map revealed as you go (nearest, an information block) hides parts of a map, and it misses games whose whole purpose is that one player hides something or themselves and the others hunt for it.
- Games in this book that use it: Hide-and-Seek (p. 251), Hunt the Thimble with its warm and cold hints (pp. 251–252), Huckle Buckle Bean Stalk, where the finder keeps quiet and sits down (p. 252), Scavenger Hunt, where teams search for hidden items from a list (pp. 279–280).
- ? text: Someone hides, or hides something. The others hunt for it, and the game ends when it is found.

## Proposed block: stop on a signal
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: stop-on-a-signal
- Why no block covers it: Call and response (nearest) is a call answered by a move, and it misses games where everyone moves freely until a sudden signal makes them stop or freeze, and whoever reacts too slowly is caught.
- Games in this book that use it: Musical Chairs, where the music stops and players grab seats (p. 277); Red Light, Green Light, where players freeze when the traffic light turns round (p. 279). Simon Says (p. 280) is a close relative, as the players act only on a marked call.
- ? text: Everyone keeps moving until a signal comes, such as the music stopping. Then you must freeze or grab a seat, and the slowest player is caught.

## Proposed block: capture and convert
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: capture-and-convert
- Why no block covers it: Already proposed in the file elias-garfield-gutschera_characteristics-of-games.06.md; this is an addition to that proposal, not a second block. Area control counts who holds places, and it misses a player who is hit or caught changing sides.
- Games in this book that use it: Dodge Ball, where a hit player stands in the circle and helps the other side (pp. 239–240); Red Rover, where a player who fails to break through joins the other team (p. 279); Ball Tag, where a correct guess lets a side take a player from the other line (p. 238).
- ? text: As already proposed: a piece or player that is captured does not leave. It changes sides and plays for the one who captured it.

## Proposed block: matching the open ends
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: matching-open-ends
- Why no block covers it: Tile placement builds a shared map, and it misses games where each new tile must match the number at the end of a growing line, so a player may have no legal move.
- Games in this book that use it: the Block game (p. 161), Bergen (p. 162), Matador, where the ends must add up to ten (p. 168), Cyprus (p. 169), Tiddle-a-wink (p. 170).
- ? text: You add a tile to the end of a line, but only if its number matches the end. If you have no matching tile, you miss your turn.

## Proposed block: bet against the bank
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: bet-against-the-bank
- Why no block covers it: Bidding has players offer money to win a prize from each other, and it misses games where one player is the bank, takes everyone's stakes and pays out by fixed odds.
- Games in this book that use it: Crown and Anchor (p. 141), Strung Flowers (p. 143), Throwing Heaven and Nine (p. 144), Collecting Tens (p. 150). In Crown and Anchor the book says the bank passes to each player in turn, because the banker gains over time.
- ? text: One player is the bank. The others place their bets, the bank throws, and winners are paid at fixed odds. The bank passes round so no one keeps the advantage.

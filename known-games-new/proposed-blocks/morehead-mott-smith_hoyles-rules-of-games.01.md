## Proposed block: fewest points
- Slot: victory-condition
- Id: fewest-points
- Why no block covers it: Most points names the highest score as the winner, and it misses games where every point is a penalty and the lowest total wins.
- Games in this book that use it: Hearts (under '(Original) Hearts'), Black Lady and Omnibus Hearts, where hearts and the Queen of Spades count against you (under 'Black Lady'); Cancellation Hearts, where each penalty point is taken from a common pool (under 'Cancellation Hearts'); Domino Hearts and Spot Hearts (under 'Domino Hearts' and 'Spot Hearts').
- ? text: Every point you pick up counts against you. When the game ends, the player with the fewest points wins.

## Proposed block: passing cards
- Slot: core-mechanic
- Id: passing-cards
- Why no block covers it: Drafting has you pick one card from a hand you are passed and keep going round, and it misses handing a fixed number of your own cards to one other player before play.
- Games in this book that use it: Black Lady and Omnibus Hearts, where each player passes three cards to the left before the first trick (under 'Black Lady'); Spades, where a Nil bidder and their partner swap three cards (under 'Spades').
- ? text: Before play begins, you choose some of your cards and hand them to another player. You cannot look at the cards you will get until you have chosen.

## Proposed block: a hand laid face up
- Slot: information
- Id: dummy-hand
- Why no block covers it: Full information shows everything to everyone and hidden hands hides every hand, and neither covers one hand that every player can see while the rest stay secret.
- Games in this book that use it: Contract Bridge, where the declarer's partner lays their hand face up and the declarer plays it (under 'Contract Bridge'); Cutthroat Bridge, Trio and Towie, where an extra hand is spread on the table (under 'Bridge for Three'); Honeymoon Bridge, where each of two players sees the hand opposite them (under 'Honeymoon Bridge').
- ? text: One hand is spread face up for all to see, while every other hand stays secret. A player may play the open hand as well as their own.

# Schafkopf — one player takes the spare cards, two play against

- From: Schafkopf (Sheepshead), the Bavarian card game, in the form played in the United States
- Players: 3 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in Bavaria
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Schafkopf'
- This version: The first player to reach 6 game points wins, not 10. The three-player game only.

Three players each get ten cards, and two cards lie face down as the widow. The first player in turn who wants the widow takes it and becomes the Player, playing alone against the other two. All queens, all jacks and all diamonds are trumps.

## What it shows a designer

A game where the teams are made fresh each hand: one against two, decided by who takes the widow. It shows how a spare hand and a fixed trump order can reshape every trick.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards with the twos to sixes taken out (32 cards)
- paper and a pen for game points

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal 10 cards to each player. Put 2 cards face down in the middle as the widow.
2. **Take the widow.** From the dealer's left, each player in turn may take the widow, or pass. Whoever takes it is the Player. They add the two cards to their hand, then put any two cards face down aside. Those cards count for them at the end. The other two players are the Opponents. If nobody takes it, play the hand as Least (see step 6).
3. **Trump order.** The trumps rank from high to low: queens of clubs, spades, hearts, diamonds; then jacks in the same order; then the diamonds ace, ten, king, nine, eight, seven. The other suits (clubs, spades, hearts) rank ace, ten, king, nine, eight, seven.
4. **Play tricks.** The player left of the dealer leads. Everyone has to follow the suit led if they can. Queens and jacks count as trumps, not as their printed suit. Any card may be played if you cannot follow. The highest trump wins, or the highest card of the suit led if there is no trump. The winner leads next.
5. **Count.** Ace is 11 points, ten is 10, king is 4, queen is 3, jack is 2, others none. There are 120 in all. The Player needs 61 or more. The Player scores 2 game points for 61–90, 4 for 91 or more, and 6 for every trick. If the Player falls short, they lose 2 game points for 31–60, 4 for fewer than 31, and 6 for taking no trick.
6. **Least.** If all three pass, nobody takes the widow. The aim is to win as few points as possible. The widow goes with the last trick. Taking every trick loses 4 game points. Taking no trick wins 4. Otherwise the fewest points wins 2.
7. **Win.** The first player to reach 6 game points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did you look for in your hand before taking the widow?
- How did being one against two change the way you played the early tricks?
- Why do you think the card values add up to a round 120?

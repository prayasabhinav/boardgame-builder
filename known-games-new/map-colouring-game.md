# The Map-Colouring Game — colour until your rival cannot

- From: a family of Nim-like paper games, including Col by Colin Vout
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: Traditional paper game; the Col version is by Colin Vout
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 132
- This version: Start with one shared colour; the harder versions the book describes are listed in the last question

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players share a drawn map. Each turn one region is coloured in, but two regions that touch along an edge may not both be coloured. The first player who cannot colour a region loses.

## What it shows a designer

The board is made by the players, and the only rule is a ban on neighbours. It shows how one small restriction turns drawing into blocking, and how adding colours changes how hard the game is to read.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with an irregular map drawn on it: 15 to 25 regions of different shapes
- one pencil, or two of different colours

## How to play

1. **Draw the map.** Draw a map of regions on the paper. Make them touch each other in different ways, like the states on an outline map.
2. **Colour.** Take turns. On your turn colour in any region that is empty.
3. **One limit.** You may not colour a region that touches a coloured region along an edge. Touching only at a corner point is allowed.
4. **Lose.** If you have no region you may colour on your turn, you lose. The other player wins.
5. **Try a harder map game.** Play again with two or three colours. Now touching regions may both be coloured if their colours differ. The first player with no legal move still loses.

## After playing, talk about

- Which region was the best one to colour first, and what made it so?
- What changed when you had several colours to choose from?
- What would change if each player had only their own colour?

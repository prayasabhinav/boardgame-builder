# Cribbage — count to thirty-one, then count your hand

- From: Cribbage, a card game that two people usually play
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 25–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 69–73
- This version: Scores are kept on paper, first to 31 points (the book uses a peg board and 61), and the substitute-card double-run rule is left out

Two players score points in two ways. First they take turns laying cards on a pile, keeping a running total up to 31, and score for pairs, 15s and runs as they go. Then each shows their hand and counts the combinations in it. A small extra hand, the crib, belongs to the dealer.

## What it shows a designer

Scoring comes from the shape of the cards, not from winning a trick. It also shows how a game can have two scoring phases that pull a player two ways: cards worth keeping for the show may be the wrong cards to play.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards
- paper and a pen to keep both scores

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut for the deal; the lowest card deals. Deal 5 cards to each player. The player who did not deal scores 3 at once.
2. **Make the crib.** Each player puts two cards face down to one side. These four cards are the crib, and it counts for the dealer.
3. **The start card.** The non-dealer cuts the rest of the deck. The dealer turns up the top card of the lower pile. If it is a Jack, the dealer scores 2.
4. **The play.** The non-dealer lays down a card and says its value. The dealer lays one and says the new total. Kings, Queens, Jacks and Tens count 10; the others count their number. Keep alternating. You cannot go over 31.
5. **Scoring in play.** Make exactly 15 and score 2. Play a card with the same number as the last card and score 2; a third in a row scores 6; a fourth scores 12. Play the third or later card of a run in any order, such as Four, Two, Five, Three, and score the length of the run. Scores for different combinations at once are added together.
6. **Go.** If you cannot play without passing 31, say "Go". The other player may play if able. The last player to play scores 1, or 2 for making exactly 31. Then the play ends and the rest of the cards are not played.
7. **The show.** The non-dealer shows their three cards first and counts them together with the start card. Then the dealer counts their hand, and then the crib.
8. **Counting a hand.** Each different set of cards totalling 15 scores 2. Each pair scores 2. A run of three or more scores one point for each card. A hand made entirely of one suit scores 3, or 4 if the start card is of that suit too. A crib scores a flush only when the start card is of its suit, and then it scores 5. A Jack of the same suit as the start card scores 1.
9. **Next hand.** Swap who deals and play again. The first to reach 31 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which two cards did you put in the crib, and did it depend on who was dealing?
- When did you play a card that scored nothing, and why?
- How did the scores of the two phases pull against each other?

# Head Detective — guess who is on your forehead

- From: Head Detective (also called The Post-it Game), a party game
- Players: 2–12 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; a party game popular in Australia in the 1980s
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Head Detective'
- This version: Everyone writes names on notes before the game, and the game ends when one player has guessed

Each player wears the name of a famous person on their forehead and cannot read it. Everyone else can. You ask questions that only need a yes or a no, and you work out who you are from the answers.

## What it shows a designer

A game can hide one fact from one player while everyone else sees it. It shows how a secret held about you, not by you, turns every answer into a clue you must piece together.

## What you need

- sticky notes or small pieces of paper, one for each player
- pens
- tape, if the notes are not sticky
- a bowl or hat to draw from

## How to play

1. **Write names.** Each player writes the names of well-known people or story characters on notes. Choose people everyone in the group knows. Fold the notes into the bowl.
2. **Stick on.** Each player draws one note without reading it. Another player sticks it on their forehead, or on a hat, so they cannot see it.
3. **Ask.** On your turn, ask the group one question that has a yes or no answer, such as "Am I a real person?" or "Do I have dark hair?"
4. **Keep the turn.** If the answer is yes, you ask again at once. If the answer is no, your turn ends and the next player asks.
5. **Guess.** At any point in your turn you may name who you think you are. A wrong guess ends your turn.
6. **Win.** The first player to name their person correctly wins. You may keep going until everyone has guessed.

## After playing, talk about

- Which questions cut down the list of people the most?
- How did getting to ask again after a yes change the way you asked?
- What would change if you could not see the other players' notes either?

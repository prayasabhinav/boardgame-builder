# The Block Game — dominoes in a line until nobody can play

- From: Dominoes, a game from China brought to Italy in the fourteenth century
- Players: 2 to 4 players
- Time: 15 min
- Made by: Traditional; first reported in Europe from Italy
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 161
- This version: First to 50 points wins. The book's game is 121, scored on a cribbage board. A hand also ends when one player plays their last tile, and that player scores as in a blocked game, because the book does not say what happens then

Players take turns adding a domino to a growing line. The end you add to must match the number at that end of the line. If you cannot match, you lose your turn. The hand ends when nobody can play, and the player holding the fewest spots scores.

## What it shows a designer

A single matching rule makes every tile useful for some moves and useless for others. The line on the table limits what you can do next, so each player changes the other players' choices without touching them.

## What you need

- a set of 28 dominoes (double-six), or 28 paper slips, each divided in two halves and marked with two numbers from 0 to 6
- paper and a pencil for scores

## How to play

1. **Pick the leader.** Mix the tiles face down. Each player takes one. The highest double leads. If no one has a double, the tile with most spots leads.
2. **Deal.** Put the tiles back and mix them again. With two players, each takes seven tiles. With three or four players, each takes five. Keep your tiles hidden. The rest stay unused.
3. **Lead.** The leader puts down one tile.
4. **Match.** The next player puts a tile at one end of the line, so that the touching halves show the same number. If you have no matching tile, you miss your turn.
5. **Block.** If nobody can play, the hand is blocked. Everyone adds up the spots on the tiles still in their hand.
6. **Score.** The player with the fewest spots scores the spots of all the hands together, their own included. If a player plays their last tile first, they score the same way.
7. **Play on.** Mix and deal again. The first player to reach 50 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which tiles did you keep back, and which did you play at once?
- When you could not match, what did that tell the others about your hand?
- How would the game change if you could draw tiles from a pile?

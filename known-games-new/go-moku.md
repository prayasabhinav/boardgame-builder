# Go Moku — five in a row on a small board

- From: Go Moku, a game from Japan
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in Japan, and in England from the 1870s
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Go Moku'
- This version: Played on an 8 by 8 grid, with 12 counters each, as the book describes for the small board

Two players put counters on the squares of a board, one at a time. The first to get five of their own counters in a straight line wins. If all counters are down and nobody has won, the counters start to move.

## What it shows a designer

A game of making a row is easy to learn, but the book says the player who goes first can force a win on a large board. It shows how a designer limits the board and the number of pieces to keep a game open.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with an 8 by 8 grid, or a chessboard
- 12 counters of one colour and 12 of another

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Draw an 8 by 8 grid of squares. Each player has 12 counters of one colour.
2. **Place.** Take turns putting one counter on any empty square.
3. **Five wins.** The first player to have five of their own counters side by side in a line wins. The line can run across, up and down, or on a slant.
4. **Slide.** If all 24 counters are down and nobody has five in a row, take turns moving one of your counters to a neighbouring empty square, in any direction.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you build your own line and when did you stop theirs?
- Why would a bigger board help the player who goes first?
- What did sliding counters change after all of them were down?

# Pente — five in a row, with captures

- From: Pente, a game from Japan's Ninuki-Renju, popular in the 1980s
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: Traditional (from Ninuki-Renju, Japan); sold as Pente in the 1980s
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 52
- This version: A 13×13 grid of lines on paper, and ten captured stones (five pairs) win

Two players take turns putting one stone on a crossing of a grid. A player wins by lining up five of their stones, or by capturing five pairs of the other player's stones.

## What it shows a designer

Making a line and defending a pair pull in different directions. A line of stones is what you want, but stones next to each other can be taken. It shows how one extra rule, capture, changes a simple building game into a fight.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a grid of 13 lines by 13 lines
- about 40 counters of one kind and 40 of another, such as coins and buttons

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Stones go on the crossings, not inside the squares. Agree who goes first.
2. **Place.** Take turns putting one stone on any empty crossing. A stone never moves once placed.
3. **Make a line.** Five of your stones in a straight line, across, up and down, or on a diagonal, wins at once.
4. **Capture a pair.** If exactly two of the other player's stones touch along a line, and you hold the crossing at each end of that line, take the pair off the board.
5. **Only you can make the sandwich.** If you place a stone so that your own pair lands between two enemy stones, your pair is safe. A capture only happens when the capturing player makes the move.
6. **Escape a threat.** If one end of your pair is already held by the enemy, add a third stone on the other end. A line of three cannot be captured in the basic game.
7. **Count captures.** The first player to capture five pairs (ten stones) also wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did building a line make your stones easy to capture?
- Which was quicker for you, five in a row or five pairs taken?
- How would the game change if lines of three could be captured too?

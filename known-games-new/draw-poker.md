# Draw Poker — bet on hidden cards, then swap some

- From: Draw Poker, a card game
- Players: 2–8 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in the United States
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Draw Poker'
- This version: Five deals, no minimum hand needed to open, a bet limit of 5 chips, and most chips at the end wins

Each player gets five cards that only they can see. There is a round of betting, then a chance to swap cards, then a second round of betting. The best hand among the players still in takes all the chips in the middle.

## What it shows a designer

The cards are the same for everyone, but what each player does with them is not. It shows how a game can run on what people think you hold rather than on what you hold.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- about 30 chips, coins or dried beans for each player
- a sheet of paper that lists the hand order, highest first: straight flush (five in a row, one suit), four of a kind, full house (three of one rank and two of another), flush (five of one suit), straight (five in a row), three of a kind, two pairs, one pair, highest card

## How to play

1. **Ante.** Everyone puts one chip in the middle. Choose a dealer. Shuffle and deal five cards face down to each player, one at a time.
2. **First betting.** The player on the dealer's left goes first. You may check (bet nothing) or bet up to 5 chips. Once someone has bet, each player in turn must call (match the bet), raise (match it and add more), or drop out and lose the chips they put in.
3. **Keep going round.** If everyone checks, move on. Otherwise betting ends when every player still in has put in the same amount.
4. **Draw.** Starting on the dealer's left, each player still in may throw away any number of cards face down and take the same number from the top of the pack. A player who wants no new cards says so.
5. **Second betting.** Bet again as before.
6. **Showdown.** Players still in show all five cards. The highest hand takes the middle. Tied hands share it.
7. **Next deal.** The deal passes to the left. After five deals, the player with the most chips wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you bet more than your cards deserved, and did anyone believe you?
- What did another player's number of drawn cards tell you?
- How would the game change if everyone's cards were face up?

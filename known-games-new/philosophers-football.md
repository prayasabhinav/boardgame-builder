# Philosopher's Football — jump the ball home

- From: Philosopher's Football (also called Phutball), a two-player grid game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: Elwyn Berlekamp, John Conway and Richard Guy; first printed in Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays (1982)
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 112
- This version: A smaller grid of 11 lines across and 15 lines down, to make the game shorter

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

One ball sits in the middle of a grid. On each turn a player either adds a stone or jumps the ball over stones. The first to bring the ball to or over the other player's goal line wins.

## What it shows a designer

Both players use the same stones, so a chain built for you also helps the other player. It shows how a shared piece makes every move both a help and a risk.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a grid of 11 lines across and 15 lines down
- one button or dark counter for the ball
- about 30 coins or counters for the stones

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Put the ball on the centre crossing. The two short edges of the grid are the goal lines. Players sit at opposite ends, each facing the goal line they defend.
2. **Your turn.** Do one thing: either put a stone on any empty crossing, or move the ball by jumping.
3. **Jumping.** The ball may jump in any direction (across, down or diagonal) over a straight run of stones, and lands on the first empty crossing beyond them. Every crossing it jumps over must hold a stone.
4. **Take the stones.** Every stone jumped over is taken off the board at once.
5. **Jump again.** If the ball lands where it can jump again, in the same direction or a new one, it may. Further jumps are optional, and so is jumping at all.
6. **Goal lines.** You win when the ball lands on, or jumps over a stone on, the goal line you are heading for. If you jump the ball onto your own goal line, you have to jump it off again in the same turn or you lose.
7. **Plan.** Experienced players delay jumping and build a chain of stones towards their goal line first, but any stone you add can help the other player too.

## After playing, talk about

- Which stones did you place to help yourself, and who else could use them?
- When did you decide to jump instead of adding another stone?
- How would you change where the ball starts to make the game level for two unequal players?

# Shout It Out — each person one word

- From: Shout It Out!, a crowd game for two teams
- Players: 10 or more players, in two teams
- Time: 15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 130

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each team makes up a phrase with one word for each player. The teams stand far apart. Each team shouts its phrase together, and the other team tries to hear what it is.

## What it shows a designer

All words arrive at once, so the other team must pick a phrase out of noise. It shows how a simultaneous action turns a shared message into a puzzle.

## What you need

- a wide space where the teams can stand far apart
- paper and a pencil to keep score

## How to play

1. **Make teams.** Make two teams of at least five players. Stand the teams as far apart as the space allows.
2. **Make phrases.** Each team chooses a phrase with exactly as many words as there are players. Each player gets one word.
3. **Shout.** On a signal, one team shouts its words all at once. The other team has to say what the phrase is.
4. **Try again.** If the guess is wrong, the shouting team may shout up to two more times.
5. **Score.** A right guess on the first shout earns 3 points, on the second 2 points, on the third 1 point. If the phrase is never guessed, the shouting team earns 1 point.
6. **Swap.** Teams swap roles. After an agreed number of rounds, the team with the most points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What made a phrase easy or hard to hear?
- How did your team decide where to stand and in what order?
- Why does the score fall with each extra shout?

## Its blocks

- Core mechanic: call and response
- Victory condition: most points
- Player interaction: team vs team
- Chance or skill: mostly skill
- Timing: everyone at once

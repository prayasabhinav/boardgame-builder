# The Royal Game of Goose — sixty-three spaces of dice

- From: The Royal Game of Goose, an old spiral-board game that came to England from the continent
- Players: 2–6 players
- Time: 20 min
- Made by: Traditional; the printed rules in the book say it was invented at the Consistory in Rome
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, pp. 15–16
- This version: Counters stand for the stakes, the geese sit on spaces the class chooses, and the rule on being overtaken is my reading of the old wording

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players throw two dice and put their counter on the space with that number. After that they add each throw to where they stand. The first to land exactly on space 63 wins. On the way there are a bridge, an ale house, a well, a maze, a prison and a space called death, each with its own price. Later makers copied the plan to teach children history, geography and astronomy.

## What it shows a designer

Players only throw and move, yet the track is full of events. It shows how special spaces can do all the work of a game with no choices, and how the same plan can carry facts when the spaces are changed.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper
- two dice
- one counter for each player, and twelve small counters or beans each for stakes
- a pen to number the spaces

## How to play

1. **Draw the track.** Draw a winding track or a spiral of 63 numbered spaces. Mark 6 as the bridge, 19 as the ale house, 31 as the well, 42 as the maze, 52 as the prison and 58 as death. Draw a goose on about nine other spaces of your choice.
2. **Start.** Each player starts with twelve stake counters. Every stake you pay goes into a pool in the middle. The winner takes the pool.
3. **First throw.** Throw both dice and add them. Put your counter on the space with that number. On later turns, add your throw to the space where you stand.
4. **The bridge.** If you land on 6, pay one stake and go on to 12.
5. **A goose.** If you land on a goose, move forward again by the same throw.
6. **The ale house.** On 19, pay one stake and miss your next throw.
7. **The well.** On 31, stay until every other player has thrown twice. If someone else lands on 31 first, that player stays and you go to the space they came from.
8. **The maze.** On 42, pay one stake and go back to 29.
9. **The prison.** On 52, pay one stake and stay. You are freed when another player lands on 52. That player takes your place in the prison.
10. **Death.** On 58, pay one stake and go back to the start.
11. **Being overtaken.** If a player lands on your space, you go back to the space they came from. Both of you pay one stake.
12. **The end.** You have to land on 63 by an exact throw. If you pass it, go back to the start. The first player to land on 63 wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Nobody chose a move. Which space gave the game its most memorable moment?
- Which space cost the most, and did it change how you felt about the dice?
- What would you put on the spaces if the game had to teach something?

# Contract Bridge — promise a number of tricks, then keep the promise

- From: Contract Bridge, a card game that grew out of Whist
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 25–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; first played in England, with the contract form dating from 1926
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Contract Bridge'
- This version: Four deals, no doubling and no dummy hand shown, and a simple score of mine in place of the full table

Two partnerships bid for the right to name the trump suit, each bid being a promise to win a number of tricks. The side with the highest bid then tries to keep that promise while the other side tries to stop it.

## What it shows a designer

Bidding and playing are two different games joined together. The first asks what you can promise from cards you can only partly see. The second asks whether you can deliver it. It shows how a promise made in public can change how the whole table plays.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Partners sit opposite each other. The dealer gives each player thirteen cards, one at a time.
2. **Bid.** Starting with the dealer, each player in turn passes or bids. A bid names a number from one to seven and a suit or no trumps, and promises to win that many tricks beyond the first six. Each bid must be higher than the one before: more tricks, or the same number in a higher suit. The order of suits from low to high is clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades, no trumps.
3. **End the auction.** The auction ends when three players in a row pass after a bid. The highest bid is the contract. The player on the winning side who first named that suit is the declarer. If all four pass, redeal.
4. **Play.** The player to the declarer's left leads any card. Everyone must follow suit if they can. The highest trump wins a trick, or else the highest card of the suit led. The winner of a trick leads the next.
5. **Score the deal.** If the declarer's side won at least as many tricks as the contract needs, it scores 20 points for each trick it promised and 10 for each extra trick. If it fell short, the other side scores 30 for each missing trick.
6. **Play four deals.** Pass the deal to the left. The side with the higher total after four deals wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did your partner's bid tell you, and what did it tell the other side?
- When did you bid higher than your cards needed, and why?
- How did the score make you want to bid low or high?

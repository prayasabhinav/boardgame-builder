# Skully — flick a bottle cap round the boxes

- From: Skully (Skelly, Bottlecaps), a New York street game
- Players: 2 to 6 players
- Time: 15 min
- Made by: Traditional; played on city pavements in the United States
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 192
- This version: A grid of 13 numbered boxes drawn in chalk, with the middle box as the skull; coins or buttons in place of weighted bottle caps

Players flick a bottle cap from box to box in number order. Hitting another player's cap earns extra boxes. The first to reach the middle box and come back out wins.

## What it shows a designer

Hitting an opponent's piece helps you as well as hurting them. It shows how a race and an attack can be the same move.

## What you need

- chalk or masking tape to draw a grid about three feet wide
- a bottle cap or button for each player, weighted with a coin taped inside if you can

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Mark 12 boxes in a ring, numbered 1 to 12, with the skull (13) in the middle. Draw a starting line a few inches from box 1. The area between the skull and the ring is No Man's Land.
2. **Flick.** From behind the line, flick your cap with your middle finger towards box 1. If it does not land fully inside, the next player goes.
3. **Move up.** If your cap lands inside a box with no part touching a line, you go on to aim for the next number, still on your turn. A miss ends your turn.
4. **Hit.** From box 2 onward, if you hit another cap, you move up a box as if you had flicked into it, and you keep your turn. Two caps hit in one flick give two boxes.
5. **No Man's Land.** A cap that stops there stays stuck until another player hits it out.
6. **Win.** Reach the skull, then go back through the numbers to box 1. The first player to do so wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you aim for a box and when for another cap?
- How did No Man's Land change where you aimed?
- What would you add so a player far behind has a chance?

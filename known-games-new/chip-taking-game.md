# Chip-taking game — everyone takes from the leader

- From: A thought example in Characteristics of Games, used to show how a game turns political
- Players: 3 to 6 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Described by Elias, Garfield and Gutschera as an imagined example
- Rules from: Characteristics of Games, p. 48

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player starts with ten chips. On your turn you take one chip from any player and throw it away. The last player with chips wins. There is almost nothing to decide except whom to hurt, so the game is mostly talk.

## What it shows a designer

When players can hurt anyone they like, the real game becomes persuading others to hurt someone else. It shows how a game with lots of targeting and little else can hide every other rule under social pressure.

## What you need

- 10 chips, coins or buttons for each player
- a bin or a pile for thrown-away chips

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Sit in a circle. Give each player ten chips and keep them in front of you where everyone can see them.
2. **Take.** On your turn, pick any other player. Take one chip from their pile and put it in the discard pile.
3. **Talk.** Talking is allowed at any time. Beg, promise, threaten or explain why someone else is the right victim.
4. **Out.** A player with no chips is out. Out players keep watching and may still talk.
5. **Win.** The last player with chips wins. Play a few rounds and change who starts each time.

## After playing, talk about

- Who did the table attack first, and why them?
- What did you say to make others pick someone else?
- If you added one rule, what would give players a reason to choose one victim over another?

## Its blocks

- Core mechanic: negotiation; choose your target
- Victory condition: last standing

- Theme: abstract

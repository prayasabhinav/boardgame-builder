# Refusal Noughts and Crosses — the other player can say no

- From: Refusal games, a rule change for two-player strategy games, first described for chess
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 5–10 min
- Made by: Described by R. Wayne Schmittberger, who says it works with almost any two-player strategy game
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 231
- This version: Noughts and crosses on a 3 by 3 grid, played as a best of five

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players play noughts and crosses with one change. Each time you choose a square, the other player may refuse it. You then have to pick a different square, and that one must be accepted.

## What it shows a designer

A single added rule can change who controls a move. It shows what happens when your opponent has a say in your turn: you choose knowing the choice can be taken away.

## What you need

- paper and a pencil

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Draw two lines across and two lines down to make nine squares. One player is X and the other is O.
2. **Say your move.** On your turn, say which empty square you want to write your letter in. Do not write yet.
3. **Accept or refuse.** The other player says yes or no. If yes, write your letter there.
4. **If refused.** Choose a different empty square. This second choice cannot be refused, so write your letter there.
5. **Win a game.** The first player with three of their letters in a row, across, down or along a diagonal, wins the game.
6. **Match.** If the grid fills with no line, nobody wins that game. The loser of a game starts the next. Play five games.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you refuse a move, and what made you sure?
- Did you ever pick a move you hoped would be refused?
- Plain noughts and crosses usually ends in a draw. What did refusing do to that?

# Hasami Shogi — trap pieces between two, make five

- From: Hasami Shogi, a board game from Japan
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in Japan on a quarter of a Go board
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 97
- This version: The book lists jumps and landing squares but not how a piece slides, so here a piece slides any number of empty squares along its row or column; Black goes first

Each player has 18 pieces in the two rows nearest them on a 9 by 9 board. A piece is taken when the other player traps it between two of their pieces. The first player to line up five pieces wins.

## What it shows a designer

One game can ask for two different things at once: to build a line and to take pieces. It shows how a threat to capture can force a player to give up a line they were making.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a 9 by 9 grid of squares
- 18 coins of one kind and 18 buttons of another kind

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Each player fills the two rows of squares nearest them with their pieces, nine in each row.
2. **Move.** Take turns moving one of your pieces in a straight line, along a row or a column, over empty squares. Pieces never move diagonally and never land on an occupied square.
3. **Jump.** A piece may also jump over an adjacent piece, of either colour, and land on the empty square straight beyond. It cannot jump over an empty square and a piece in one move.
4. **Trap.** If your move leaves one of the other player's pieces between two of yours, in a row or in a column, take it off. A piece may safely move into a space between two enemy pieces. A piece in a corner is taken when your pieces block both squares next to it.
5. **Win.** The first player with five of their own pieces in a straight line wins. The line can run along a row, a column or a diagonal, and it cannot lie in the two rows where that player's pieces began.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you give up a line to save a piece, or give up a piece to keep a line?
- Why does a piece in a corner need more care?
- How did the empty middle of the board change the way you moved?

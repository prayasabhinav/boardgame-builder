# Ship, Captain, Mate and Crew — find three numbers in order, then score the rest

- From: Ship, Captain, Mate and Crew, an old dice game played by sailors
- Players: 2 to 6 players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 136
- This version: The book's rule about who shoots first in the next round is left out, and a player who misses 6, 5 and 4 scores nothing, which the book does not say

Each player gets three throws of five dice. You must find a 6, then a 5, then a 4, in that order. If you do, the two dice left over are your score. Every extra throw can raise that score, or lower it.

## What it shows a designer

A rule that says "first this, then that" turns a pile of dice into a small plan. It also shows how a second chance can be a risk: you may throw again, but you must keep what comes up.

## What you need

- five ordinary dice
- paper and a pencil to write each player's score

## How to play

1. **Order.** Each player throws one die. The highest goes first and the lowest goes last. If two players tie, they throw again.
2. **Find the ship.** On your turn you have three throws. Throw all five dice. You need a 6 first. Put a 6 aside if you get one.
3. **Find the captain and the mate.** Throw the dice that are left, looking for a 5, then a 4. Put each aside as it comes. A 4 does not count until you hold the 6 and the 5. If a 6 and a 4 come up together, only the 6 can be put aside.
4. **Count the crew.** Once you hold 6, 5 and 4, the two dice still in your hand are your crew. Add them up for your score.
5. **Throw again if you like.** If you have throws left, you may throw the two crew dice again to try for more. Whatever shows after your last throw is your score, even if it is lower.
6. **If you run out.** If your three throws end before you hold 6, 5 and 4, your score for the round is nothing.
7. **Win the round.** When everyone has played, the highest crew score wins. If the top two tie, throw the round again.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you throw the crew dice again, and when did you stop?
- How did the rule "6, then 5, then 4" change which dice you kept?
- Nobody could touch anyone else's dice. What would change if they could?

# War — the highest card takes the pile

- From: War, a children's card game
- Players: 2 or more players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Characteristics of Games, p. 278
- This version: Stop after 15 minutes if nobody has all the cards, and the biggest pile wins. When two cards tie, both stay on the table and go to whoever wins the next round. The book does not give a tie rule.

A deck is shared out at random. Each round, everyone shows one card at the same moment. The highest card wins the cards on the table. Play on until one player holds everything.

## What it shows a designer

Nobody chooses anything, yet people still play. It shows what a game feels like when luck does all the work and the only decision is to keep going.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 playing cards
- a table big enough for a pile of cards in the middle

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Shuffle and deal the whole deck face down, as evenly as you can. Keep your cards in a pile. Do not look at them.
2. **Show.** Every player turns over one card from their pile at the same moment.
3. **Win the round.** The highest card takes all the cards on the table. Put them under your pile.
4. **Ties.** If the top cards match, leave them on the table and show again. The winner of that round takes all of them.
5. **Run out.** When your pile is empty, shuffle your won cards and use them as a fresh pile.
6. **Out of the game.** A player with no cards is out. The last player with cards wins, or the biggest pile at 15 minutes.

## After playing, talk about

- Which moment made you feel something, if there was no choice to make?
- What would you add so that a player could decide something?
- How long was too long, and what made it feel that way?

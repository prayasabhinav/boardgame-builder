# All-Fours — four ways to score from one hand

- From: All-Fours (also called Old Sledge and Seven-Up), a traditional card game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 35
- This version: Two players, no penalties for playing the wrong suit (take the card back and play again), and the book's four-player partner game left out

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player gets six cards, and one more card is turned up to name the trump suit. They play six tricks. Up to four points can be won in a hand, and the first player to seven points wins.

## What it shows a designer

One hand can give out several different prizes: the top trump, the bottom trump, the Jack of trumps and a count of card values. It shows how naming separate things to win changes what a card is worth, so that a single Jack can be worth a trick and a point at once.

## What you need

- a pack of 52 playing cards, Ace high and Two low
- paper and a pen to keep the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut the cards, and the higher card deals. The dealer gives each player six cards, three at a time, then turns up the next card. Its suit is the trump suit.
2. **Jack turned up.** If the turned-up card is a Jack, the dealer scores one point at once.
3. **Stand or beg.** The player who is not dealing looks at the hand and says "I stand" to keep it, or "I beg" to ask for a change.
4. **Answer a beg.** The dealer either gives the other player one point, or gives each player three more cards and turns up a new trump. If the new trump is the same suit, repeat. If the cards run out, deal again.
5. **Play tricks.** The player who is not dealing leads any card. The dealer plays one card to it. The higher card of the suit led wins, but any trump beats a card of another suit. You must follow suit if you can, or play a trump. The winner leads next.
6. **Score four points.** High: the player dealt the highest trump scores one. Low: the player dealt the lowest trump scores one, even if the other player wins it. Jack: whoever wins the trick holding the Jack of trumps scores one. Game: each player adds up the cards they won, Ace 4, King 3, Queen 2, Jack 1, Ten 10. The higher total scores one, and a tie goes to the player who was not dealing.
7. **Next hand.** The deal passes to the other player. The first to seven points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which of the four points did you plan for, and which just arrived?
- When did you beg, and what did it cost the dealer to answer?
- How did one card, such as the Jack of trumps, change what you did with the rest of your hand?

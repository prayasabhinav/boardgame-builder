# Super Babel — trade your way to ten cards

- From: Super Babel, a party game by Robert Abbott
- Players: 6 or more players, best with 15 or more, plus one scorekeeper
- Time: 20 min
- Made by: Robert Abbott
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 146
- This version: Only the one-for-one trades and the five-card poker hands are used, and the list of hands is shortened

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player holds ten cards and trades with everyone else, card for card, trying to turn the ten into two poker hands. A player who finishes hands them in, gets ten new cards and starts again.

## What it shows a designer

There is no turn order at all. Everyone bargains at the same moment, and finishing a hand just starts the next. It shows how a race against a clock turns into a market when every player needs what another holds.

## What you need

- playing cards: one deck for about every three players
- a scorekeeper who does not play, with a score sheet and a pencil
- a clock set to 20 minutes
- a chart of hands and points for every player

## How to play

1. **Deal packets.** Shuffle the decks together and deal them into piles of ten cards. Give each player one pile.
2. **Trade.** Everyone trades with everyone, whenever they like. Trades must give the same number of cards each way. Bargain aloud or in private, alone or with allies.
3. **Go out.** You go out when your ten cards make two five-card hands, each a straight or better (a straight is five cards in a row). Points: straight 5, flush 7, full house 10, four of a kind 15, straight flush 20, royal flush 21.
4. **Hand in.** Give your ten cards to the scorekeeper, who writes your points and gives you a new pile of ten. Go on trading.
5. **Finish.** When the clock runs out, the scorekeeper adds up each player's points. The highest total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Who traded openly and who kept cards secret? Which worked better for them?
- What made a trade worth accepting when it did not help you at all?
- How would the game change if trades could be of unequal sizes?

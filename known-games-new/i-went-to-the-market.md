# I went to the market — remember the growing list

- From: I Went to the Market, a spoken memory game
- Players: 2 or more players, most fun with at least 6
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 75
- This version: Played forwards through the alphabet only, without the backwards round

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players sit in a circle and build one list of things bought at a market, one item for each letter of the alphabet. Each player repeats the whole list and adds an item. Anyone who forgets an item is out.

## What it shows a designer

The only thing on the table is a list in everybody's head. It shows how a game can make each turn harder than the last without any new rule, because the list gets longer.

## What you need

- nothing; players sit in a circle in one place

## How to play

1. **Start.** The first player says "I went to the market to buy" and names something that begins with A, such as artichokes.
2. **Repeat and add.** The next player repeats the list and adds an item beginning with B. The one after repeats both and adds a C item, and so on through the alphabet.
3. **Stuck for a letter.** If you cannot think of an item and nobody else can, skip that letter. If another player can, you are out and the next player uses their item.
4. **Forgotten.** A player who leaves out or mixes up an item in the list is out.
5. **Win.** The last player left wins. If several reach the end of the alphabet, you may play the list again from Z back to A.
6. **Memory trick.** You may picture each player doing something with their item, such as eating an artichoke, to help the list stay in mind.

## After playing, talk about

- What helped you remember the list, and what made you lose it?
- How did the list change what you chose to add?
- How would a game that adds one item each turn but never removes players feel different?

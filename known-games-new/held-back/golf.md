# Golf — clear the rows, up or down by one

- From: Golf, a solitaire that can be played as a contest
- Players: 1 player, or two comparing results
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Golf'
- This version: Played for a single hole, with the score compared by two players if they like

Thirty-five cards lie face up in seven columns. You turn cards from the pack one at a time onto a single pile. You may move an outer card from the layout onto that pile if it is one rank higher or lower. Your score is how many cards are left in the layout.

## What it shows a designer

All of the cards you are fighting are in plain view, and still the order of the pack decides what can be done. It shows how a game with no hidden hands can still offer a puzzle.

## What you need

- one deck of 52 cards
- a pencil and paper to note scores

## How to play

1. **Lay out.** Deal seven columns of five cards, face up and overlapping so you can read each card. Keep the other 17 cards in a face-down pack.
2. **Start the pile.** Turn the top card of the pack face up. It begins the pile.
3. **Play from the layout.** You may move the uncovered card of a column onto the pile if it is one rank higher or lower than the top card. Suits do not matter.
4. **Ranks.** The ace is low and the king is high. You can go up and down, such as 2, ace, 2. But nothing goes on a king, so a run stops there.
5. **Turn another.** When you have no move, turn the next card of the pack onto the pile. You may go through the pack only once.
6. **Score.** When the pack is used up, count the cards left in the layout. A lower number is a closer result. Two players can play with their own packs and compare.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you stop playing a queen so a king could come later?
- Which column did you clear first, and why?
- What did you do differently from the other player?

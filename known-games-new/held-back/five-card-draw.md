# Five-Card Draw — bet on a hand you can change once

- From: Five-Card Draw, the basic game of poker
- Players: 2–8 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: not named in the book
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 304
- This version: Beans or coins for chips, five hands, and the player with the most chips at the end wins

Each player holds five cards nobody else can see. Players bet on whose hand will be best, throw away some cards for new ones, and bet again. Bluffing can win a hand nobody could beat.

## What it shows a designer

The cards matter less than what the other players think your cards are. It shows how betting turns a game of luck into a game of reading people, and why a player who folds is making a choice, not losing.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 playing cards
- about 20 beans or coins for each player

## How to play

1. **Agree a limit.** Decide the most anyone may bet at one time. Keep it low.
2. **Ante and deal.** Everyone puts one chip in the middle, called the pot. Deal five cards face down to each player.
3. **Bet.** Starting left of the dealer, each player passes or bets. After a bet, each player in turn folds (drops out), calls (matches the bet) or raises (matches it and adds more). If everyone passes, deal again.
4. **Draw.** If more than one player is left, each may throw away cards and take the same number of new ones. A player who wants none says so by knocking on the table.
5. **Bet again.** Bet as in step 3. The round ends when every bet has been answered.
6. **Show.** Players still in turn their cards up. The best poker hand takes the pot. A pair beats nothing, two pairs beat a pair, then three of a kind, a run of five, five of one suit, a full house, and four of a kind.
7. **Play on.** Play five hands. The player with the most chips wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you bet on a weak hand, and why?
- What did you learn about a player from the number of cards they drew?
- How did the limit on bets change how bold people were?

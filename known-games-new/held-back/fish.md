# Fish — ask for a rank, collect books of four

- From: Fish (Go Fish), a children's card game
- Players: 2–5 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Fish'
- This version: Your turn carries on after you draw a card of the rank you asked for, a rule the book says children often add

Players ask each other for cards of one rank, such as sevens. If the player asked has any, they hand them all over and the asker goes again. If not, they say Go Fish and the asker draws a card. Four cards of one rank make a book.

## What it shows a designer

Every question gives away a little of your own hand. It shows how asking, not trading, can be the main move of a game, and how memory of what others asked becomes a skill.

## What you need

- a pack of 52 cards
- a table to sit round

## How to play

1. **Deal.** With two players, deal seven cards each. With more players, deal five each. Put the rest face down in the middle as the stock.
2. **Ask.** On your turn, name another player and a rank, such as: Lucy, give me your sevens. You must hold at least one card of that rank.
3. **Give or fish.** If the player has cards of that rank, they must give you all of them. Ask again. If they have none, they say Go Fish, and you draw the top card of the stock. Then the turn passes to the left.
4. **Make a book.** When you hold all four cards of one rank, show them and put them in front of you.
5. **Win.** When the cards run out, the player with the most books wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did you learn about other players' hands from their questions?
- When did you ask for a rank you held only one of, and why?
- How would the game change if you could name the suit as well?

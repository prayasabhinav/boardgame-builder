# Pyramid — pairs that make thirteen

- From: Pyramid, a solitaire for one player
- Players: 1 player
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Pyramid'
- This version: As in the rules, with no redeal

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Twenty-eight cards form a triangle. You remove two uncovered cards at a time whose values add up to thirteen. A king is removed on its own. You win if the whole pyramid is cleared.

## What it shows a designer

A sum is a simple matching rule that anyone can learn in a minute. It shows how a shape on the table, which cards are covered, limits what you can do and when.

## What you need

- one deck of 52 cards
- a flat table

## How to play

1. **Build the pyramid.** Lay 7 rows: one card in the first, two in the second overlapping the first, and so on to seven cards in the last row. Keep the other 24 cards as the hand.
2. **Values.** Ace is 1, jack 11, queen 12, king 13. Other cards count their number.
3. **Pairs.** Remove any two available cards that total 13. Available means not covered by another card. Take kings out alone when they are uncovered.
4. **The hand.** Turn the hand over one card at a time onto a waste pile. The top waste card and the card just turned over can both be used in a pair.
5. **No redeal.** Go through the hand once. You win if you clear the whole pyramid.

## After playing, talk about

- Which card did you wait on, and why?
- What did the shape of the pyramid make you plan for?
- How much of the result was the order of the pack?

# Checkers — jump to capture, reach the far row

- From: Checkers (English draughts), a board game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Checkers'
- This version: Played on the dark squares only, with kings shown by stacking two pieces. The book's tournament opening restrictions are left out

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players move twelve pieces each across the dark squares of an eight by eight board. A piece captures by jumping over an enemy piece into the empty square behind it. A piece that reaches the far row becomes a king and can move backward too.

## What it shows a designer

A capture that you are forced to make turns the other player's plan into a trap for you. It also shows how one rule, being crowned, changes what a piece is worth from then on.

## What you need

- a board of 8 by 8 squares, such as a chessboard
- 12 light counters and 12 dark counters
- 4 spare counters, to put on top of a piece when it becomes a king

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Use only the dark squares. Each player puts 12 counters on the dark squares of the three rows nearest to them. The dark player moves first.
2. **Move.** A counter moves one square diagonally forward onto an empty dark square. Players take turns.
3. **Jump.** If an enemy counter is diagonally in front of yours and the square behind it is empty, jump over it into that square and take the enemy counter off the board.
4. **Keep jumping.** If your counter can jump again from where it lands, it must carry on in the same turn. It may change direction, but only forward.
5. **A jump is compulsory.** If you can jump, you may not make any other move. If you can jump in two ways, you choose which.
6. **Crown.** A counter that reaches the far row becomes a king: put a second counter on top. A king moves and jumps backward as well as forward. If a counter reaches the far row by jumping, it stops there for that turn.
7. **Win.** You win when the other player has no counters left or cannot move.

## After playing, talk about

- Which jump did you not want to make, and why were you forced into it?
- When did you give up a counter on purpose, and what did you get for it?
- How did a king change the way you looked at the board?

# Accordion — fold the row into one pile

- From: Accordion, a very short solitaire
- Players: 1 player
- Time: 5 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Accordion'

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

You deal cards in a row, one at a time. A card can move onto the card to its left, or the third card to its left, if they share a suit or a rank. You win if all 52 cards end in one pile, which is rare.

## What it shows a designer

A game can be over in minutes and have almost no choices. It shows what a rule looks like when the deal does nearly all of the work, and it lets a class talk about what a player still decides.

## What you need

- one deck of 52 cards
- a long table

## How to play

1. **Deal a row.** Lay cards face up from left to right, one at a time.
2. **Move.** After each card, check it. If it has the same suit or rank as the card to its left, or the third card to its left, move it onto that card. A pile moves as one card, with its top card counting.
3. **Chain moves.** One move can open another. Keep checking after every move.
4. **Choose.** Sometimes two moves are possible. Pick the one that leaves the fewest piles.
5. **End.** When the whole pack is dealt and no move is left, count the piles. One pile is a win.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you have a real choice?
- How many piles were left, and could any move have reduced them?
- Is a game with almost no choices still a game?

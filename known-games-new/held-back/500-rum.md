# 500 Rum — score for every card you lay down

- From: 500 Rum, a rummy game where melds earn points
- Players: 2–5 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 319
- This version: Three hands, and the player with the most points wins

You build groups of cards on the table as in rummy, but now each card laid down scores points, and each card left in your hand costs points. You may take cards from deep in the discard pile.

## What it shows a designer

Points for what you put down make a hand a trade between scoring now and getting out. It shows how adding a score to every meld changes a rummy game from a race into a calculation.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 playing cards
- paper and a pencil for scores

## How to play

1. **Deal.** With two players, deal thirteen cards each; with more, seven each. Put the rest face down as the stock. Turn one card up and spread the discard pile sideways so every card shows.
2. **Draw.** Take the top card of the stock, or any card from the discard pile. If you take a card that is not on top, you must also take every card above it, and you must use the card you chose at once in a meld.
3. **Meld and add.** Lay down sets (three or four of a kind) or runs (three or more of one suit in order; an ace may be low or high, but a run cannot go round from king to ace to two). You may add a card to any meld on the table; keep it in front of you for scoring.
4. **Discard.** End your turn by throwing one card on the discard pile.
5. **Go out.** The hand ends when someone puts down their last card.
6. **Score.** Each player adds the card values they put on the table and subtracts the values left in their hand. An ace scores 1 in a run with a two and three, and 15 otherwise. Face cards score 10; other cards their number.
7. **Play on.** Play three hands. The highest total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When was it worth taking a deep pile of discards?
- How did scoring each laid-down card change when you chose to go out?
- Which cards did you hold back, and what did that risk?

# Commerce — swap one card for a table card

- From: Commerce, an old card game for any number of players
- Players: Any number of players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 66
- This version: The three-card form, with a knock to end the swapping

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player holds three cards, and three more lie face up in the middle. On your turn you may swap one of yours for one on the table, or pass. When someone is happy, they knock, and everyone else gets one more swap. Then all hands are shown.

## What it shows a designer

A tiny hand and a tiny table make every swap matter. The player who knocks tells everyone the hand is ready, and gives the rest exactly one chance to catch up.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards
- a few counters for each player, to use as chips

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal 3 cards to each player, one at a time. Turn 3 more cards face up in the middle.
2. **Swap or pass.** Starting left of the dealer, each player may swap one card from their hand with one on the table, or pass. A player who passes cannot swap again.
3. **Knock.** When you are happy with your hand, rap on the table. Every other player now has one last swap or pass. Then everyone shows their hand.
4. **Rank of hands.** From highest to lowest: three of a kind (Aces highest, then Kings, down to Twos); a run of three in a row (Ace-King-Queen highest, Three-Two-Ace lowest); three cards of one suit (the one with the highest total wins); a pair plus any other card; the highest total of pips (Ace counts 11, picture cards 10, others their number).
5. **Win.** The player with the highest hand takes one chip from each other player. If two hands tie for highest, each takes a chip from the other players.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you knock, and what did that tell the others about your hand?
- Did the three cards on the table change what you decided to keep?
- How would the game change with five cards instead of three?

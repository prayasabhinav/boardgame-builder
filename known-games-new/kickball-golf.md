# Kickball Golf — kick the ball at the tree

- From: Golf, played with the foot and a light ball
- Players: 1 or more players
- Time: 15 min
- Made by: Robert Schmittberger, who made it up for his own lawn
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 152
- This version: Holes are won by the lowest number of kicks, and the player with the most holes wins; the book also allows a lowest total score

Players kick a ball at a short course of targets, such as tree trunks or large tins. Each target is a hole. The player who hits a target in the fewest kicks wins that hole.

## What it shows a designer

Distance, target size and the ground set how hard each hole is, and a par is just a number someone chose. It shows how a game of one's own hands and feet can be balanced by changing the course instead of the rules.

## What you need

- a light ball about 25 cm across, such as a pre-inflated beach ball
- four or five targets in an open space: tree trunks, large tins or buckets
- a paper and pencil for the score

## How to play

1. **Mark the course.** Choose targets and a starting spot for each hole. Make some holes long and some short. Keep well away from windows, cars and small children.
2. **Set a par.** Decide how many kicks each hole should take. Change it later if everybody finds it too easy or too hard.
3. **Kick.** Everyone starts at the same spot and kicks in turn. Bare feet work better than shoes. Use a hard swing for distance and a gentle push for short shots.
4. **Mark the ball.** If one ball is in another player's way, mark its spot with a leaf, lift it, and put it back after the other kicks.
5. **Hit the target.** A hole is finished when the ball touches the target. Count the kicks. The player with the fewest kicks wins the hole. Break a tie with a sudden-death play-off from the first hole.

## After playing, talk about

- Which hole was hardest, and was it the distance or the target?
- How did you change your kick for long and short shots?
- How would you change the course to make it fairer for a weaker kicker?

# Charades — act it out without a word

- From: Charades, a party game
- Players: 4 or more players, in two teams
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 283
- This version: Team play only, with a timer for each turn and a referee who does not play

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

A referee writes ideas on slips of paper: songs, films, actions, famous people. One player at a time acts out an idea for their own team. No words, no letters shown with the fingers, no props. The team has a short time to guess.

## What it shows a designer

A game can take away speech and still be full of talk, because the guessers may shout. It shows how one player who knows the answer, a clock, and a ban on words make a game out of simple things.

## What you need

- small slips of paper and a pen
- a timer or watch
- one person to be the referee and keep time

## How to play

1. **Write the ideas.** The referee writes one idea on a slip for each player. Ideas can be nouns or actions, and can follow a theme such as films or songs.
2. **Deal the slips.** Hand each player one slip. Players keep their slip secret from everyone.
3. **First team.** The referee chooses which team goes first. One player from that team stands and acts out their slip.
4. **No words.** The actor may not speak, spell with the hands, or use props. The actor may act out the whole idea or one part of it.
5. **Guess.** Only the actor's own team guesses. If they are right in time, the next player from the same team goes.
6. **Time out.** If time runs out, the turn passes to the other team.
7. **Winner.** The first team in which every player has had a turn wins. The referee settles any argument.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you show something that has no obvious action?
- Did the clock make you act faster, or act worse?
- What would change if the actor's team could also ask yes-or-no questions?

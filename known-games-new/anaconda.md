# Anaconda — pass three cards, then reveal them one by one

- From: Anaconda, a Poker game often chosen in Dealer's Choice
- Players: 3–6 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Anaconda'
- This version: Three deals, a bet limit of 3 chips, and most chips at the end wins

Each player gets seven cards, passes three to a neighbour, and keeps the best five. Then you set your five cards in a fixed order and turn them over one at a time, with betting after each.

## What it shows a designer

You choose the order in which your own hand becomes public, and you cannot change it afterwards. It shows how a plan made in secret turns into a trail of clues for the others.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- about 30 chips, coins or dried beans for each player
- a sheet that lists the hand order, highest first: straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pairs, one pair, highest card

## How to play

1. **Ante and deal.** Everyone puts in one chip. Deal seven cards face down to each player.
2. **Pass.** Each player chooses three cards and passes them face down to the player on their left. Then each player throws away two cards, leaving five.
3. **Bet.** Bet once, with the usual choices: check, bet, call, raise or drop out.
4. **Set the order.** Each player stacks their five cards face down in the order they will turn them over. The order cannot change.
5. **Turn and bet.** Everyone turns over their top card. Bet. Turn over the second card. Bet. Carry on until each player still in has shown all five cards.
6. **Showdown.** The best five-card poker hand takes the middle. Ties share it.
7. **Next deal.** The deal passes left. After three deals, the player with the most chips wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which card did you choose to show first, and why?
- What did the three cards you received tell you about your neighbour?
- When did the order of your cards matter more than the cards themselves?

# The Hyena Game — mothers walk to the well

- From: Li'b el Merafib (the Hyena Game), a game of the Baggara Arabs of the Sudan
- Players: 2 or more players
- Time: 25–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; played by the Baggara Arabs of the Sudan, with the track drawn in sand
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 13
- This version: A spiral of 24 spaces on paper and three coins in place of split sticks

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player's marker is their mother, walking from a village along a spiral to a well and back. Some throws do not move her at all. They go into a store of tokens called tabas, which pay for washing at the well, for the missing steps of a short throw, and later for the hyena. The first mother home lets a hyena loose, and the player who owns it chases down the other mothers.

## What it shows a designer

A throw can be used now or saved for later. It shows how a store of saved points turns a game of luck into one of timing, and how the winner can be given a new role that acts against everyone else.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper for the spiral
- three coins, for the three split sticks (call heads the white side)
- one marker for each player, and one more marker for the hyena
- beans or small counters to keep the tabas on, where the book marks them down on the sand

## How to play

1. **Draw the track.** Draw a spiral of 24 spaces on paper. Mark the outside end as the village, and a bigger circle at the centre as the well. Each space is a day's walk.
2. **Set out.** Each player puts their mother marker in the village.
3. **Throw.** On your turn toss the three coins and count the heads. One head is a taba: take a bean for your store. Two heads score 2. Three heads score 3. No heads score 6.
4. **Keep throwing.** You keep throwing until you throw two heads. That score is played and your turn ends.
5. **Leaving the village.** Your mother cannot leave the village until you have thrown a taba. A taba never moves her. After that, a score of 2, 3 or 6 moves her that many spaces along the spiral. Two mothers can share a space.
6. **The well.** Your mother needs an exact throw to land in the well. If a throw is too short to reach, you can pay one taba for each space still missing.
7. **Washing.** At the well, she needs two tabas to wash her clothes and two more to start back. If you do not have them, she waits. While she waits, write down any 2, 3 or 6 you throw and use them later.
8. **The way home.** She comes back along the spiral in the same way, and the first mother back in the village lets the hyena loose.
9. **The hyena.** The hyena belongs to the player whose mother got home first. It leaves the village when its owner pays two tabas. Every score it throws counts double. At the well it has to pay ten tabas for a drink before it goes on. After drinking it still moves at double speed and eats any mother it overtakes. It cannot eat before it has drunk.
10. **Winning.** The player who becomes the hyena wins. A player whose mother is eaten loses by more than one whose mother gets home safe.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you save a score for later, and when did you spend it at once?
- What did it feel like to be the one chased by the hyena?
- What would change if the winner got no new role at all?

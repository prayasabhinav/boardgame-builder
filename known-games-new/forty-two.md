# Forty-Two — bid for tricks, with dominoes for cards

- From: Forty-Two, a trick-taking game played with dominoes
- Players: 4 players, in two teams of two
- Time: 20 to 25 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 163
- This version: Four players in two teams. Each tile belongs to the suit of its higher number, and the teams play three hands, adding up the points. The book's rule for the order of play is kept simple

This is a card game played with dominoes. Players bid how many points their team will win. The top bidder picks the trump suit and leads. Each trick is won by the best tile, and counting tiles carry extra points.

## What it shows a designer

Bidding and trick-taking fit together: the bid sets a target, and the tricks show whether the target was reasonable. A bid is a promise made before you know where the tiles are.

## What you need

- a set of 28 dominoes (double-six), or 28 paper slips marked with two numbers from 0 to 6
- paper and a pencil for scores

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Mix the tiles face down. Each of the four players takes seven. Partners sit opposite each other.
2. **Bid.** The player holding the 1-0 tile bids first. Each player gets one turn to bid or pass. The lowest bid is 30, and each bid must be higher than the one before. The most that can be won in a hand is 42.
3. **Pick trumps.** The highest bidder says which suit is trumps. The eight suits are blanks, doubles, and ones to sixes.
4. **Lead and follow.** The bidder leads any tile. Everyone else must play a tile of the same suit if they can. A tile's suit is the higher of its two numbers, unless it belongs to the trump suit. A double is the highest tile of its own number.
5. **Win the trick.** The highest tile of the suit led wins the trick. A trump tile beats it, and a higher trump beats a lower one. The winner leads the next trick.
6. **Count.** Each trick won is worth 1 point. Five of the tiles carry extra points: 5-5 and 6-4 are worth 10 each, and 5-0, 4-1 and 3-2 are worth 5 each. Together with the seven tricks, this makes 42.
7. **Score.** If the bidders' team wins at least what they bid, they score their bid plus the points they won. If not, the other team scores the bid plus what they won.
8. **Play on.** Play three hands. The team with the higher total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did you look at in your seven tiles before you bid?
- How did your partner's play tell you what they held?
- A bid is a promise. What happened to teams who promised too much?

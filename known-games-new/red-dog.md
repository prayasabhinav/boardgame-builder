# Red Dog — bet against the top card

- From: Red Dog (also called High-Card Pool), a quick betting card game
- Players: 2–10 players
- Time: 10–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Red Dog'
- This version: Play for buttons or beans, with a fresh start when the pot is empty

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player holds five cards and bets chips from a shared pot that the top card of the pack can be beaten. To beat it, you show a higher card of the same suit. A win takes chips out of the pot, and a loss adds your bet to it.

## What it shows a designer

You choose how big a risk to take, and you can count the cards you have seen to guess the odds. It shows how a bet that you size yourself gives a chance game some feeling of control.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards, with the ace as the highest card
- about 10 coins or buttons for each player
- a pot space in the middle

## How to play

1. **Ante.** Every player puts one chip into the pot.
2. **Deal.** Deal 5 cards to each player, face down (4 each if more than 8 play). The rest is the pack.
3. **Bet.** The player left of the dealer goes first. You may bet from one chip up to the whole pot. Or you may pay one chip to the pot and drop out of the turn without showing your hand.
4. **Turn.** The dealer turns up the top card of the pack. If you hold a higher card of the same suit, show it and take back your bet plus the same number of chips from the pot.
5. **Lose.** If you cannot beat it, your bet goes into the pot and you show your hand. Put your cards aside face down.
6. **Next player.** Play passes left. When the pot is empty, everyone puts in one chip again and the deal starts over.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you decide how many chips to bet?
- What could you work out from the cards you saw?
- Would a limit on bets change who took risks?

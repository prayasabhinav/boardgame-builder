# Auction Pitch — sell the right to choose trumps

- From: Auction Pitch (also called Commercial Pitch), a card game related to All-Fours
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 39
- This version: Four players, each starting on 10 points; a hand holds at most four points, so no bid goes above four; no penalties for playing the wrong suit (take the card back)

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Nobody turns up a trump card. Instead the players bid in points for the right to choose the trump suit by leading a card. A player who wins the bid must win at least that many points in the hand, or lose them. Scores count down, and the first player to reach nothing wins.

## What it shows a designer

Bidding and trick-taking sit side by side, with the bid made in the same points the game is scored in. It shows how a promise made before the cards are played turns a plain hand of tricks into a test of that promise.

## What you need

- a pack of 52 playing cards, Ace high and Two low
- paper and a pen to keep the scores

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut the cards, and the highest card deals. The dealer gives each player six cards, three at a time. No trump card is turned up. Each player's score starts at 10.
2. **Sell the pitch.** The player to the left of the dealer, the eldest hand, offers the right to choose trumps. Starting from the eldest hand's left, each other player may make one bid, in points.
3. **Decide.** The eldest hand speaks last. They either sell to the highest bidder, or keep the right and pitch the trump themselves. If nobody bids, the eldest hand must pitch.
4. **Seller's points.** A seller who accepts a bid takes that many points off their score straight away, before any card is led.
5. **Pitch.** The player with the right leads a card, and its suit becomes trump. Each other player plays one card in turn. You must follow suit if you can, or play a trump if you cannot, or any card if you hold neither. The highest card of the suit led wins, but a trump beats other suits. The winner leads next, and after the first trick the leader may lead any suit.
6. **Count four points.** High: the highest trump played. Low: the lowest trump played. Jack: the Jack of trumps. Game: the player who won cards worth the most, Ace 4, King 3, Queen 2, Jack 1, Ten 10. If two players tie for Game, nobody scores it.
7. **Settle.** A buyer who wins at least the points bid takes off those points and any extra they won. A buyer who falls short adds the bid to their own score and takes nothing off for that hand. A player who kept the pitch and falls short of the highest bid offered is set back by that bid in the same way. Other players take off the points they won.
8. **Win.** The deal passes to the left. The first player whose score reaches nothing wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did you look at in your six cards before you decided how high to bid?
- When did the eldest hand keep the pitch instead of selling, and why?
- What changed for the other three players once a bid had been made?

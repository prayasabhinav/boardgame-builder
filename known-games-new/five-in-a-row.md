# Five-in-a-Row — first to a line of five

- From: Five-in-a-Row (also called Go-Moku or Go Bang), a game played on a go board
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; played on a go board, with a 15 by 15 board as the official size
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 108
- This version: Marks are drawn with two pens on paper instead of stones on a board

Two players put stones on the crossings of a big grid, one at a time. Stones never move and are never taken. The first to make a straight line of exactly five wins.

## What it shows a designer

A very short rule set can still hold deep play. It also shows how moving first can be an advantage, and how one small limit on the first player brings the two sides closer.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a grid of 15 lines across and 15 lines down
- two pens of different colours (or crosses and circles)

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Draw 15 lines across and 15 lines down. Marks go on the crossings.
2. **First two moves.** Black goes first and marks the centre crossing. White then marks any empty crossing.
3. **A limit for Black.** Black's second mark may not go in the 5 by 5 square around the centre. It has to be at least three crossings away from Black's first mark.
4. **Take turns.** From then on, each player marks one empty crossing in turn. A mark never moves and is never taken off.
5. **Win.** Make a straight line of exactly five of your marks, across, down or along a diagonal, with no gap and no opposing mark inside it. A line of six or more does not win.
6. **Draw.** If the grid is full and nobody has a line of five, the game is drawn.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you stop building your own line and start blocking the other player's?
- Did moving first help, and what did the limit on Black's second mark change?
- What would you change in the rules to make the two sides more equal?

# Nyout — a cross-and-circle race with throwing sticks

- From: Nyout (yut), an old game from Korea
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in Korea, where a similar game is on record from the third century
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 2
- This version: Two horses each, four coins in place of the four throwing sticks, and a horse leaves by reaching or passing the exit. The text gives only the 29 marks, so the number of small marks in each place is worked out to make that total

Two players race their horses round a ring of marks and out at the top. A horse that lands on a corner mark can cut across the middle of the cross. A horse that lands on an enemy horse sends it back to the start. In Korea it was played for money in public houses, and the sticks were thrown through a ring to stop cheating.

## What it shows a designer

One throw can mean a long safe walk round the ring or a short risky cut through the middle. It shows how a shortcut on a board gives a dice game a real choice at each corner.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper
- four coins, for the four throwing sticks
- two counters of one colour and two of another, for the horses

## How to play

1. **Draw the board.** Draw 29 marks on paper. The mark at the centre and the four at the quarters are bigger than the others, and the one at the top (A) is the exit. The book gives no more of the layout, so this version arranges them as a ring with a cross inside it: call the other big marks B (left), C (bottom) and D (right), put four small marks between each pair of big ones on the ring, draw a line from B, C and D to the centre with two small marks on each, and a line from the centre to A with two small marks.
2. **Set out the horses.** Each player has two horses. Both start off the board. A horse enters on the small mark just left of A and travels anticlockwise, towards B.
3. **Throw.** Toss all four coins. Heads count as white sides. One head scores 1, two score 2, three score 3. Four heads score 4 and no heads (all tails) score 5. After a 4 or a 5, throw again before you move.
4. **Move.** Move one horse by the score. If you threw twice, you may split the scores between your two horses. Two horses of the same side may share a mark and move together.
5. **Cut across.** If a horse stops exactly on B, C or D, then on its next move it may leave the ring. It goes along the line to the centre, then along the line from the centre to A, one step for each mark.
6. **Hit.** If your horse stops on a mark with an enemy horse, that horse goes back off the board to start again. You throw again.
7. **Leave.** A horse leaves the board when a throw takes it to A or past it. The first player to take both horses out wins.

## After playing, talk about

- At a corner mark, when did you take the shortcut, and when did you stay on the ring?
- What did being hit and sent back change about how you moved your other horse?
- What did the extra throw on a 4 or a 5 do to the feel of a turn?

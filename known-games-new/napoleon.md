# Napoleon — bid how many tricks you will take

- From: Napoleon, a French card game related to Euchre
- Players: Up to 6 players, each playing alone
- Time: 20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 105–106
- This version: Six deals with chips, and if everyone passes the cards are dealt again

Each player gets five cards and bids how many tricks they can take. The highest bidder names trump and plays against everyone else together. Win your bid and the others pay you; miss it and you pay them.

## What it shows a designer

A bid is a promise made while only you can see your cards. It shows how a game can reward a player for knowing their hand and punish them for guessing too high.

## What you need

- a standard deck of 52 cards (with 4 or fewer players, take out the sevens and eights; with 7 players, add the sixes)
- about 20 chips or coins for each player

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal five cards to each player, two and then three at a time, starting on the dealer's left.
2. **Bid.** The player on the dealer's left speaks first. Say how many tricks you will take, or pass. Each player in turn may bid higher or pass. A bid of all five tricks is called Napoleon.
3. **Name trump.** The highest bidder names the trump suit and leads the first card. If they name no trump, the suit of that first card is trump.
4. **Play.** Others play in turn. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you must play a trump if you have one. The highest card of the suit led wins, but trump beats it.
5. **Stop at your bid.** A bidder who bid three may throw down their cards once they have taken three tricks. If they carry on to lead a fourth, they must now take all five.
6. **Pay.** If the bidder takes at least the tricks they bid, each opponent pays them one chip for each trick bid. A Napoleon pays ten chips from each. If they fail, the bidder pays each opponent the same.
7. **Six deals.** Pass the deal to the left. Play six deals. The player with the most chips wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you decide how high to bid?
- What does it feel like to play alone against everyone else?
- When did the other players help each other, without talking about it?

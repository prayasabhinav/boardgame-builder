# Authors — name the exact card you want

- From: Authors, a card game once played with pictures of writers
- Players: 4–6 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Authors'
- This version: Played with a plain pack, and a book is scored as one point instead of poker chips

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players ask each other for one named card, such as the ace of spades. You may only ask if you already hold another card of the same rank. If the player has it, you get it and ask again. A book is all four cards of one rank.

## What it shows a designer

Asking for one exact card tells everyone which rank you hold, but not which suit. It shows how a rule about what you must already hold shapes what you give away every time you speak.

## What you need

- a pack of 52 cards
- a pen and paper to keep the count of books

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal all the cards face down, one at a time. Players may get different numbers of cards.
2. **Ask.** On your turn, name a player and a card, such as: Joe, give me the ace of spades. You must hold at least one other card of that rank, and you may not ask for a card in your own hand.
3. **Receive.** If the player has the card, they must hand it over face up. You go on asking. When a player does not have it, your turn ends and the turn passes to the left.
4. **Make a book.** A book is all four cards of one rank. Show it as soon as you have it and put it face down in front of you.
5. **Mistakes.** If a player breaks a rule, they give one point to each other player.
6. **Win.** When all the books are made, the player with the most wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did it tell you when someone asked for a card in a rank?
- When did you ask for a card you were sure of, and when for a guess?
- How is naming one card different from asking for a whole rank?

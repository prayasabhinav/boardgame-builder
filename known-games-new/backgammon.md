# Backgammon — bring all fifteen pieces home

- From: Backgammon, a dice and board game thousands of years old
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; the book says a relative about five thousand years old was found in present-day Iraq
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 5
- This version: Drawn on paper, with no doubling cube and no extra points for winning by a wide margin; the first to bear off all fifteen pieces wins. The starting spots are the usual ones, since the book shows them only in a picture

Each player moves fifteen pieces round a track of twenty-four points, in opposite directions, using two dice. A piece alone on a point can be knocked off the board. The first player to take every piece home and off the board wins.

## What it shows a designer

The dice tell you how far, but you choose which pieces move. It shows how a game can be quite lucky and still leave room for planning, and how a hit that sends a piece back makes a race into a fight.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with 24 points drawn as triangles, 12 along the top and 12 along the bottom, and a gap in the middle called the bar
- 15 coins of one kind and 15 of another
- 2 dice

## How to play

1. **Number the points.** Number them 1 to 24 round the track. White moves from 1 up to 24 and Black moves from 24 down to 1. White's home is points 19 to 24 and Black's is points 1 to 6.
2. **Set up.** White puts 2 pieces on point 1, 5 on point 12, 3 on point 17 and 5 on point 19. Black puts 2 on point 24, 5 on point 13, 3 on point 8 and 5 on point 6.
3. **First turn.** Each player rolls one die. The higher roller moves using both numbers. After that, players take turns and roll two dice each turn.
4. **Move.** Move one piece by each die. You may move one piece twice, or two pieces once each. Double numbers let you play each die twice. You must play both numbers if you can. If you can play only one, play the higher.
5. **Landing.** You may land on an empty point or on a point with your own pieces. You may not land on a point with two or more enemy pieces. If you land on a point with one enemy piece, that piece goes to the bar.
6. **The bar.** A player with a piece on the bar can move nothing else until it comes back in. A roll of 1 puts it on the opponent's first point, a roll of 2 on the second, and so on. It cannot enter on a blocked point.
7. **Bear off.** Once all fifteen of your pieces are in your home points, you may take pieces off the board. A roll of 1 takes a piece from the first home point, 2 from the second, and so on. The first player to bear off all fifteen wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did the dice decide your move, and when did you have a real choice?
- Which of your pieces were left alone on a point, and what did that cost you?
- How would the game feel if you could never knock a piece off the board?

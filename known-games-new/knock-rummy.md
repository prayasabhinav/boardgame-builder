# Knock Rummy — keep your sets hidden and knock first

- From: Knock Rummy, the Rummy game that Gin Rummy grew from
- Players: 2–4 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in the United States
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Knock Rummy'
- This version: Hands are paid in points from a running total, and the first player to win 5 hands wins the game

You play as in Basic Rummy, but nothing is laid on the table until the end. Sets stay in your hand. Cards that fit no set are your deadwood, and the aim is to have the least of it when someone knocks.

## What it shows a designer

Keeping sets hidden removes the shared table of finished work. It shows how moving work from the table back into the hand makes a Rummy game about guessing, not about adding to each other's sets.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- a pen and paper to keep score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal 10 cards each with two players, 7 each with three or four. Put the rest face down as the stock and turn the top card face up beside it.
2. **Each turn.** Draw the top card of the stock or the top discard. Then discard one card. No one lays down sets.
3. **Card values.** Face cards count 10, aces count 1, other cards count the number on them. Aces are low only.
4. **Knock.** At any turn you may knock, which ends the play. Everyone shows their hand, with sets set apart from deadwood.
5. **Score.** Add up each player's deadwood. If the knocker has the lowest total, each other player pays the knocker the difference. If another player ties the knocker, that player wins instead. If another player is lower, that player wins, and the knocker pays an extra 10.
6. **Win.** Play more hands. The first player to win 5 hands wins the game.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you knock because your hand was low, and when because you guessed theirs was higher?
- What did you learn from the cards others took from the discards?
- How is hiding your sets different from laying them down?

# Two-minute alphabet — talk through the alphabet

- From: Two-Minute Alphabet, a spoken scene game
- Players: 2–6 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional; the book links it to the TV show Whose Line Is It Anyway?
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 58
- This version: Players share one score, and a new topic starts after a stuck turn

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players act out a short conversation on a topic they have picked. Every sentence must begin with the next letter of the alphabet. Together they try to reach Z inside two minutes.

## What it shows a designer

A tight rule on how to speak lets players make up a scene together without a plot. It shows how a game can be cooperative and still leave one player alone with the pressure of a stuck turn.

## What you need

- a watch with a second hand, or a phone timer
- paper and a pencil for the score

## How to play

1. **Pick a topic.** Choose a place or a situation, such as going to the chiropractor, being saved by a lifeguard or registering to vote.
2. **Pick a letter.** Choose any letter to start. The first sentence begins with it.
3. **Talk in turns.** Players take turns speaking, as the people in the scene. Each sentence begins with the next letter of the alphabet. After Z, go back to A.
4. **Stuck.** A player who cannot think of a sentence in time is stuck.
5. **Score.** Players have two minutes to finish all 26 letters. If they manage it, everyone scores 10. If a player gets stuck, that player scores minus 1, and the game starts again with a new topic and letter.

## After playing, talk about

- What helped you find the next sentence when the letter was awkward?
- How did the scene change when one player kept getting hard letters?
- What would the game be like without the two-minute limit?

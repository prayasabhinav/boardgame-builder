# Tiddlywinks — flick a counter into the cup

- From: Tiddlywinks, a table game from England
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Joseph Fincher filed the first patent for it in 1888; Cambridge students made it popular in the 1950s
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 35
- This version: Two players with four coins each and one bigger coin as a shooter, and a fixed ten-minute limit

Each player flicks small coins towards a cup by pressing the edge of a bigger coin on them. A coin in the cup scores most. Coins can also be flicked on top of an opponent's coin to trap it.

## What it shows a designer

A game decided by your hands has no dice and no hidden cards, yet nobody can repeat a shot exactly. It also shows how one rule, covering a coin to trap it, turns a flicking game into a fight.

## What you need

- 8 small coins, 4 of one kind and 4 of another
- 2 bigger coins or buttons, one per player, as shooters
- a cup, jar lid or shot glass
- a flat table and a watch

## How to play

1. **Set out.** Put the cup in the middle of the table. Each player puts their four coins at their own corner of the table.
2. **Choose who starts.** Each player flicks one coin towards the cup. The coin that lands nearest the cup starts. Then put the coins back at the corners.
3. **Flick.** Press the edge of your shooter down on the edge of a coin so that it jumps up and forward. Coins are played from where they land.
4. **Keep your turn or pass it.** A coin that lands in the cup, or balances on the rim, counts as potted and you go again. Otherwise the turn passes.
5. **Trap and send off.** A coin with another coin lying even slightly on top of it cannot be played until it is free. A coin that leaves the table goes back to its owner's corner for the next turn.
6. **Finish.** Play stops when one player pots all four coins or when ten minutes have passed. Each potted coin scores 3 and each free coin on the table scores 1. Trapped coins and coins never played score nothing.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you flick at the cup, and when did you flick on top of the other player's coin?
- Which part of your score came from a shot you could not repeat?
- What did the time limit change about how you chose your shots?

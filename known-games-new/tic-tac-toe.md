# Tic-tac-toe — three in a row before the other player

- From: Tic-tac-toe (noughts and crosses), a paper game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 2–5 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 82
- This version: A best-of-five match, with the loser of each game going first in the next

Two players take turns writing X and O in a grid of nine squares. The first to write three of their own letters in a straight line wins. Because both players see everything, careful players usually draw.

## What it shows a designer

A game this small can be fully understood in a few plays. It shows what happens to a game once both players can see all of it: choices stop being a surprise and every game tends to draw.

## What you need

- paper and a pencil

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Draw two lines across and two lines down to make nine squares.
2. **Choose letters.** One player is X and the other is O.
3. **Take turns.** Write your letter in any empty square. Players alternate.
4. **Win a game.** The first player with three of their letters in a row, across, down or along a diagonal, wins the game.
5. **Next game.** The loser of a game starts the next one. If the grid fills up with no line, nobody wins.
6. **Match.** Play five games. Whoever wins more games wins the match.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you stop attacking and start blocking?
- Why do careful players keep drawing, and what would stop that?
- What happens to the game if the first player to make a line loses, or if the grid is 4 by 4?

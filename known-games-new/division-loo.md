# Division Loo — share a pool, three cards each

- From: Division Loo, an English card game for five or seven players
- Players: 5 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 79–81
- This version: The rule that the leader must play the highest trump is dropped

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each hand has just three tricks. Before play, everyone decides whether to play or give up their cards. Players who play try to win tricks. Each trick won earns a third of a pool of chips. A player who plays and wins nothing has to put chips in for the next pool.

## What it shows a designer

A pool that is paid for by failure and shared out by success gives every hand a stake. Giving up a hand costs nothing, but a spare hand (the dummy) tempts you to change your mind.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards, ranked Ace high down to Two
- about 15 counters for each player, to use as chips

## How to play

1. **The pool.** The dealer puts 3 chips in the middle. Agree beforehand what they are worth. The pool must always divide by 3.
2. **Deal.** Deal 3 cards to each player, one at a time, and a spare hand of 3 cards, the dummy, to the middle. Turn up the next card: its suit is trumps.
3. **Declare.** Starting left of the dealer, each player says "I play" or "I pass" before looking at their cards. A player who says "I play" may swap their hand for the dummy, with the first player to declare getting the first choice. Whoever takes the dummy must play it. A player who passes gives their hand to the dealer, who puts it aside.
4. **Play.** The first player leads a card. Everyone who is playing puts one card on it. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you must play a trump if you have one. If you have neither, play any card. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, or the highest trump if any were played.
5. **First trick.** The winner of the first trick leads a trump if they have one.
6. **Share out.** After three tricks, each trick won earns a third of the pool. Win one trick for a third, two for two thirds, three for the whole pool.
7. **Failing.** A player who said "I play" and wins no trick puts 3 chips in the next pool. The dealer adds the usual 3 to the next pool only if nobody owes chips.

## After playing, talk about

- What did you look for in your hand when it was your turn to declare?
- Did taking the dummy feel like a gamble or a rescue?
- How did a growing pool change who was willing to play?

# Coffeepot — guess the verb with a stand-in word

- From: Coffeepot, a spoken guessing game
- Players: 3–8 players
- Time: 5–10 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 69
- This version: One person guesses at a time, with no limit on the number of questions

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

One player leaves the room while the rest pick a verb. The player comes back and asks questions, saying "coffeepot" in place of the verb. The group answers yes, no or I don't know.

## What it shows a designer

A single nonsense word can stand in for the hidden thing, so players can ask about it without naming it. It shows how a guessing game can be made friendly by letting the answers teach.

## What you need

- nothing; a room or corridor where one player can wait outside

## How to play

1. **Choose a verb.** One player is It and leaves the room. The others pick a verb, such as sleep, dance, shop or ski.
2. **Return.** It comes back and asks questions that use the word "coffeepot" in place of the verb, such as "Do people coffeepot outdoors?" or "Can you coffeepot sitting down?".
3. **Answer.** Someone answers each question truthfully with yes, no or I don't know. Answers go round the group in turn.
4. **Guess.** It may try to name the verb at any time.
5. **Next round.** When It guesses right, the person who answered the last question becomes It.
6. **Harder.** You may use a verb with an object, such as brush your teeth or ride a horse.

## After playing, talk about

- Which questions told you the most, and which only repeated what you knew?
- How did "coffeepot" make asking easier than naming the verb?
- What would change if you could answer only yes or no?

# Nine Men's Morris — make a mill, take a piece

- From: Nine Men's Morris, a board game found in Egypt, Sri Lanka, Norway and Ireland
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; a board was cut into the roof of a temple at Kurna in Egypt about 1400 BC, and another was found in a Viking ship about AD 900
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 93
- This version: The board is drawn in the usual pattern of three squares, and if every one of the other player's pieces is in a mill, you may take any one of them

Two players each have nine pieces. First they place the pieces one at a time on the points of the board, then they slide them along the lines. Three of your pieces in a row is a mill, and a mill lets you take one of the other player's pieces off the board.

## What it shows a designer

A game can have two parts that feel quite different: first placing, then moving. What you do in the first part decides how free you are in the second, and the aim of making a row is the same throughout.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with the board drawn on it (see step 1)
- 9 coins of one kind and 9 buttons or coins of another kind

## How to play

1. **Draw the board.** Draw three squares, one inside the next. Join the middle of each side of the outer square to the middle of the same side of the inner square with a straight line. The corners and the middle of every side are the 24 points.
2. **Place.** Take turns putting one piece on any empty point, until each player has put down all nine.
3. **Make a mill.** Three of your pieces in a row along one line is a mill. Each time you make one, take one of the other player's pieces off the board, but not one that is part of a mill.
4. **Slide.** When all the pieces are down, take turns moving one of your pieces along a line to the next point, if it is empty. Making a mill still lets you take a piece.
5. **Win.** You win if the other player cannot move any piece, or if they are left with only two pieces.

## After playing, talk about

- While placing, did you build for your own mills or stop the other player's? How did you choose?
- When did a blocked piece matter more than a lost piece?
- Did the placing part and the sliding part feel like one game or two?

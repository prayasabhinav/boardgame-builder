# Crossword Squares — the same letters, a different grid

- From: Crossword Squares, a paper-and-pencil word game
- Players: 2 players (more can play)
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional; the book says it goes back at least several decades and has been sold under several names
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 128

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player has a secret 5 by 5 grid. Players take turns calling a letter, and everyone writes that letter in their own grid. After 25 letters, the grids are scored for words.

## What it shows a designer

Everyone gets the same pieces but makes a different result. It shows how a game can keep players apart and still make them care about what the others call.

## What you need

- paper and a pencil for each player
- a 5 by 5 grid drawn by each player, kept hidden from the others
- a dictionary, or an agreement on which words count

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Each player draws a 5 by 5 grid and keeps it hidden.
2. **Call a letter.** The first player calls out any letter of the alphabet and writes it in any empty square of their own grid.
3. **Everyone writes it.** At the same time, every other player writes that same letter in an empty square of their own grid.
4. **Pass the call.** The next player calls a letter. Everyone writes it. Go on until every grid has 25 letters.
5. **Count words.** A word counts if it reads across or down in an unbroken line of squares in one row or column. The row PARWN holds PAR but not PAWN.
6. **Score.** A three-letter word scores 10, a four-letter word 20 and a five-letter word 40. Words that overlap score separately. If one word sits wholly inside a longer one, such as CAT inside CATS, only the longer word counts.
7. **Scores.** Each player works out their own total. The book does not say who wins, so agree before you start, for example that the highest total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When you called a letter, were you thinking of your own grid or the others'?
- Where did you keep a square empty for later, and did it help?
- Why does it score twice as much for each extra letter?

## Its blocks

- Core mechanic: pattern building; making words from letters
- Victory condition: most points
- Player interaction: solo in parallel
- Chance or skill: mostly skill
- Theme: abstract
- Information visibility: hidden hands
- Timing: take turns

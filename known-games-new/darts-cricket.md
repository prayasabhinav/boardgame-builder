# Darts Cricket — close the numbers, then score on them

- From: Cricket, a darts game from England
- Players: Any number of players, in two equal teams
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; the book says it began in England and is played in bars and family rooms
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 226
- This version: Coins tossed onto a sheet of paper in place of darts; seven boxes, with no double or triple scoring; written for two players, where the book has any number in two equal teams

Players take turns tossing three coins at a sheet of paper with seven numbered boxes. Hit a number three times to close it. After that, each further hit on it scores points, until the other player has closed it too.

## What it shows a designer

A score can open and close as the game goes on. It shows how a number you have closed becomes a weapon, because the other player can no longer score on it.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper, drawn with seven boxes labelled 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 and Bull
- three coins of the same size for each player
- a second sheet and a pencil for the score
- a line on the floor or table about one metre from the sheet

## How to play

1. **Draw the target.** Draw seven boxes on the sheet, large enough that a coin can land inside one. Label them 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 and Bull. Lay it flat on the table.
2. **Toss.** On your turn, stand behind the line and toss your three coins one after another. A coin counts for the box it mostly covers. A coin that lands on a line, lands outside the sheet or bounces off is a dead throw.
3. **Close numbers.** Make a mark on the score sheet for each hit. Three hits on a number close it for you.
4. **Score.** Once you have closed a number and the other player has not, each further hit on that number scores its face value. Bull is worth 25 for each hit.
5. **Stop scoring.** When both players have closed a number, nobody scores on it any more.
6. **Win.** The first player to close all seven numbers wins, as long as their points are equal to or higher than the other player's. If their points are lower, play goes on and they keep tossing for points on any number the other has not closed.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you aim for a number you needed, and when for one that could score?
- How did closing a number change what the other player did?
- What changed when a coin landed on a line?

# Coon Can — meld all your cards

- From: Coon Can, a card game for two
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 68

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players each try to turn all their cards into sets of three or four of a kind, or runs of cards in a row in one suit. You draw a card, lay down any set you can make, and throw one card away. The first to lay down all of their cards, and one more, wins.

## What it shows a designer

You are not attacking the other player. You are racing to arrange your own cards, and the only link between you is the discard pile, where the card you throw away may be the card they need.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards with all Eights, Nines and Tens taken out (40 cards left)

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut for the deal; the highest card deals. Deal 10 cards to each player (3, then 3, then 4). Put the rest face down as the stock.
2. **Rank.** From low to high: Ace, Two, Three, Four, Five, Six, Seven, Jack, Queen, King. The Ace is not next to the King.
3. **Sets and runs.** A set is three or four cards of the same number. A run is three or more cards in a row in the same suit.
4. **Your turn.** Draw the top card of the stock. If it helps you make a set or run, lay that set or run face up on the table. Then throw one card from your hand on the discard pile. If you do not use the drawn card, throw it on the discard pile face up.
5. **Take the discard.** Instead of drawing, you may take the card your opponent just threw away, if it lets you lay down a set or run.
6. **Win.** The first player to lay down all 10 starting cards plus one more (11 cards in all) wins. If neither can, the game is a draw and the other player deals next.

## After playing, talk about

- Which cards did you keep, and which did you throw away?
- Did you ever hold back a set to avoid showing your plan?
- How did the discard pile tell you what your opponent was building?

# Slobberhannes — dodge three tricks

- From: Slobberhannes, a trick-taking game
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 143–144

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Four players each play for themselves in a trick game with no trump. Three tricks cost a point: the first, the last, and the one holding the Queen of Clubs. The first player to reach ten points loses.

## What it shows a designer

Only three tricks matter, and you want none of them. It shows how a game can be about the Queen of Clubs for the whole hand, with everyone guessing who holds it.

## What you need

- a deck with only the cards from ace down to seven in each suit (32 cards)
- paper and a pencil to keep score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut for the first lead: the highest card leads, and the player on that person's right deals. Deal eight cards to each player, two at a time. There is no trump suit.
2. **Play.** The leader plays any card. Others must follow suit if they can, and otherwise play any card. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick. The winner leads next. Ace is highest and seven lowest.
3. **Penalty tricks.** Take a point if you win the first trick, a point if you win the last trick, and a point if you win the trick holding the Queen of Clubs.
4. **Slobberhannes.** If one player wins all three of those tricks in the same hand, they score one more point.
5. **Revoke.** If you fail to follow suit when you could, add a point to your score.
6. **Next hand.** The deal moves to the left. Keep a running score.
7. **Lose.** The first player to reach ten points loses, and pays an agreed stake, such as a sweet, to each other player.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you try to lose the first trick, the last trick or the Queen of Clubs?
- Why is the lead a risk and an advantage at once?
- What did you do when you were left holding the Queen of Clubs?

# Go Fish — ask for the card you need

- From: Go Fish, a card game
- Players: 2–5 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 107
- This version: Each player starts with five cards and the rest form the fish pile, so there is always something to draw

Players ask each other for cards of one rank, hoping to gather all four of a kind. If the other player has none, you draw from the pile and the turn usually passes.

## What it shows a designer

Asking costs nothing, but every question tells the table what you hold. It shows how a game of collecting sets also becomes a game of remembering what others asked for.

## What you need

- a standard deck of 52 cards
- a flat table with room for a face-down pile in the middle

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal five cards to each player. Put the rest face down in the middle as the fish pile.
2. **Make books.** Four cards of the same rank make a book. If you already hold a book, lay it face down in front of you before play starts.
3. **Ask.** On your turn, pick any other player and ask for a rank you hold yourself, such as "Do you have any sixes?" If they have some, they hand over every one.
4. **Keep asking.** After you receive cards, you ask again. You may ask the same player or another. This goes on until someone says they have none.
5. **Go fish.** When you are refused, take the top card of the fish pile. If it is the rank you asked for, your turn goes on. If not, your turn ends.
6. **Next turn.** The player who refused you goes next. Lay down every book as soon as you complete one.
7. **End and count.** The game ends when a player has no cards left. Everyone counts their books. The most books wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which question gave away the most about your hand?
- When did you ask a player because of something they had asked earlier?
- How much of your result came from the pile, and how much from your questions?

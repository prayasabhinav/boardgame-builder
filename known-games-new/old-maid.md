# Old Maid — don't be left holding the Queen

- From: Old Maid, a card game
- Players: 2 or more players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 114–115

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Everyone pairs off the cards they can and throws the pairs away. One card has no partner. Players draw blindly from each other's hands, and whoever is left with the odd Queen loses.

## What it shows a designer

The aim is to avoid something, not to gain it, and the whole game turns on one card passing between hands. It shows how a game can have one loser and no winner.

## What you need

- a standard deck of 52 cards with the Queen of Hearts taken out
- a flat table

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal every card out, one at a time, round the table. Some players may hold one card more than others.
2. **Pair off.** Look at your hand. Take out every pair of the same rank and put them face down in front of you. With three of a kind you can lay down only two. With four, lay down both pairs.
3. **Draw from a neighbour.** Going clockwise, each player fans their hand face down and the player next to them takes one card without looking.
4. **Match.** If the card you took makes a pair with a card in your hand, lay the pair down and take another card. If it does not, the card joins your hand and the turn passes on.
5. **Hide the Queen.** The Queen of Diamonds has no partner, because the Queen of Hearts is gone. Whoever holds it tries to pass it on.
6. **End.** The game ends when every pair is gone. The player left holding the Queen of Diamonds is the Old Maid and loses.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you try to hide that you held the Queen of Diamonds?
- Does a player have any choice about which card they draw?
- What would change if two cards had no partner?

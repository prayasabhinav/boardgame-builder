# Pick-up sticks — lift one without moving another

- From: Pick-up sticks (Jackstraws), a game played with a pile of sticks
- Players: 2–4 players
- Time: 5–10 min
- Made by: Traditional; the book traces it to Native American players or to a Chinese game called Spillikins
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 79
- This version: Twenty matchsticks, straws or pencils instead of a bought set, with one point for each stick

One player drops a bundle of sticks into a heap. Players then take turns lifting a single stick from the heap. If any other stick moves, the turn ends.

## What it shows a designer

A game can be made only of a steady hand and a shared heap. It shows how a turn that ends on the first slip keeps every player watching the others closely.

## What you need

- 20 thin sticks, such as matchsticks, straws or short pencils
- a flat table

## How to play

1. **Drop.** One player holds all the sticks upright in one hand, just above the table, and lets go so that they fall in a heap.
2. **Lift.** On your turn, try to take one stick out of the heap without touching or moving any other stick.
3. **Go again.** If you lift a stick cleanly, keep it and try for another one.
4. **Slip.** If any other stick moves while you work, your turn ends at once. The next player starts from the heap as you left it.
5. **Finish.** When the last stick is lifted, count your sticks. The most sticks wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you choose which stick to try first, and which did you leave for later?
- What did the heap look like after your slip, and who did it help?
- What would change if some sticks were worth more points than others?

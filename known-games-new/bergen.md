# Bergen — make both ends of the line match

- From: The Bergen game, a dominoes game with scoring for the shape of the line
- Players: 2 to 4 players
- Time: 15 to 20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 162
- This version: Triple headers and the special rules for players close to the end of a game are left out, and the winner of a hand scores as in the book. The game is first to 10 points with three or four players, first to 15 with two

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players add dominoes to a line by matching ends. Points come from the line itself: you score when a tile you place makes both ends of the line show the same number. Players who cannot match draw from a pile.

## What it shows a designer

Here the rule that scores is not the rule that moves. Matching tiles is how you play, but points come from the shape you leave. This pushes players to hold some tiles back and wait for the right moment.

## What you need

- a set of 28 dominoes (double-six), or 28 paper slips marked with two numbers from 0 to 6
- paper and a pencil for scores

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Mix the tiles face down. With two or three players, each takes six tiles. With four players, each takes five. The rest form a pile, face down.
2. **Lead.** The player with the lowest double puts it down and scores 2 points. If nobody has a double, the player with the lowest tile leads and scores nothing.
3. **Match.** In turn, put a tile at one end of the line so that the touching halves show the same number.
4. **Cannot match.** Draw one tile from the pile. If it fits, you may play it. If not, your turn passes. Stop drawing when only two tiles are left in the pile.
5. **Double header.** If the tile you place makes both ends of the line show the same number, you score 2 points.
6. **Going out.** The first player to play their last tile calls "Dosto" and scores 2 points. The hand ends.
7. **Blocked hand.** If the pile has two tiles left and nobody can match, the hand is blocked. The player with no doubles wins it. If that is no one, the player with the fewest spots wins. A player who wins a blocked hand scores 2 points.
8. **Play on.** Deal again. The first player to reach the target wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you keep a double back, and what were you waiting for?
- Did you ever place a tile only for the points, even though another tile was better for your hand?
- How does scoring for the shape of the line change what players do?

# Oh Hell! — win exactly the tricks you said

- From: Oh Hell!, a card game from New York card clubs
- Players: 3–7 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; first seen in New York card clubs in the late 1930s
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Oh Hell!'
- This version: Four players, and only the first six deals (one card in the first, six in the last)

Each deal, everyone says how many tricks they will win, and they only score if they win exactly that many. Winning more is as bad as winning fewer. The hands get larger every deal.

## What it shows a designer

Most trick games reward winning more. Here the target is exact, so a strong hand can be a problem. It shows how a scoring rule that punishes both too much and too little makes players watch each other's bids as closely as their own cards.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- paper and a pen for the score, with one column for each player

## How to play

1. **Deal.** In the first deal each player gets one card, in the second deal two, and so on up to six. The dealer then turns the next card face up. Its suit is trumps for the deal, except in the last deal (the sixth, in this version), when no card is turned and the hands are played with no trumps. Put the rest of the pack aside.
2. **Bid.** Starting with the player on the dealer's left, everyone in turn says how many tricks they will win. You cannot pass, but you may say zero. Write each bid down so that anyone can ask what was said.
3. **Play.** The player to the dealer's left leads. Everyone must follow suit if they can. The highest trump wins a trick, or else the highest card of the suit led. The winner leads next.
4. **Score.** A player who won exactly the number they bid scores the bid plus 10. A player who won more or fewer scores nothing.
5. **Keep going.** Pass the deal to the left and add a card to each hand. The highest total after the sixth deal wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you try to lose a trick on purpose?
- How did the last bidder's position help or hurt them?
- Why does the game get harder as the hands get bigger?

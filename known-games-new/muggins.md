# Muggins — make the ends add up to a multiple of five

- From: Muggins (All Fives), a dominoes game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 20–25 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 27
- This version: Two players with seven dominoes each, played to 50 points, with a set cut from card if you have no dominoes

Players add dominoes to the two ends of a growing line. Whenever the two open ends add up to 5, 10, 15 or 20, the player who made it scores that number. The hidden hand makes it a game of guessing what the other player holds.

## What it shows a designer

A shared line of tiles with scoring at its ends gives every move two jobs: finding a legal place and making a score. It also shows how hidden tiles let you work out what the other player cannot play.

## What you need

- a set of 28 dominoes, or 28 strips of card marked with pairs of spots from 0-0 to 6-6
- paper and a pencil for the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Turn the dominoes face down and mix them. Each player takes seven and stands them on edge so the other cannot see. The rest are the boneyard.
2. **Lead.** The first player puts any domino in the middle. If its total spots are a multiple of five, they score that total.
3. **Add to an end.** Players take turns putting a domino at either end of the line, matching spots on the touching halves. A double goes across the line.
4. **Score.** After a play, add the spots on the two open ends. A double at an end counts all its spots. If the total is a multiple of five, score it.
5. **Cannot play.** Draw from the boneyard one domino at a time until you can play. Two dominoes must always stay in the boneyard. If you still cannot play, pass.
6. **Going out.** The round ends when a player uses their last domino. They score the spots left in the other hand, rounded to the nearest five. If play is blocked, the lighter hand scores that way.
7. **Next round.** Deal again. The first player to reach 50 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which tiles did you keep for later, and which did you play first?
- What did the tiles the other player did not play tell you about their hand?
- When was drawing from the boneyard a help, and when was it a cost?

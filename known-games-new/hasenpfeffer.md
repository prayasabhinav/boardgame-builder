# Hasenpfeffer — bid how many tricks your side will take

- From: Hasenpfeffer, a bidding form of Euchre
- Players: 4 players, in two teams of two
- Time: 20–25 min
- Made by: Traditional; the name is a German word for rabbit stew
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Hasenpfeffer'
- This version: Game is 6 points, not 10. Use a joker, or a slip of paper marked JOKER, as the extra card.

Four players in two teams play with a small pack. Each player gets one chance to bid a number of tricks from one to six. The high bidder takes a spare card, names the trump suit and leads. If the bidder's side takes enough tricks, it scores them. If not, it loses points.

## What it shows a designer

A bid that is also a promise to a partner you cannot talk to. It shows how an auction with a single turn for each player pushes people to judge their own hands carefully.

## What you need

- a deck of cards with only the ace, king, queen, jack, ten and nine of each suit (24 cards) plus a joker
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Partners sit opposite each other. Deal 6 cards to each player, three at a time. Put the last card face down as the widow.
2. **Rank the cards.** The joker is the highest trump. Then comes the jack of the trump suit (right bower), then the other jack of the same colour (left bower), then ace, king, queen, ten, nine of trumps. In the other suits the order is ace, king, queen, jack, ten, nine; the left bower counts as a trump, not in its own suit.
3. **Bid.** Starting at the dealer's left, each player has one bid or a pass. A bid is a number from one to six. If all four pass, whoever holds the joker shows it and plays a bid of three.
4. **Take the widow.** The high bidder picks up the widow, names the trump suit and puts one card face down aside. They lead any card to the first trick.
5. **Play tricks.** Follow suit if you can. The highest trump wins, or the highest card of the suit led if there is no trump. The winner leads next.
6. **Score.** If the bidding side wins at least as many tricks as it bid, it scores one point for each trick it won. If not, it loses the number it bid, and may go below zero. The other side scores nothing.
7. **Win.** The first side to reach 6 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you work out what number to bid, and what did your partner learn from it?
- What did the widow change about your hand?
- When did passing make more sense than bidding?

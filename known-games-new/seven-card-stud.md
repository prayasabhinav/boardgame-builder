# Seven-Card Stud — bet while some of your cards show

- From: Seven-Card Stud, a stud form of poker
- Players: 2–10 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: not named in the book
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 306
- This version: Beans or coins for chips, three hands, and the player with the most chips at the end wins

Each player is dealt seven cards, some face down and some face up. Everyone can see part of everyone's hand. Players bet after each new card, and the best five of the seven count.

## What it shows a designer

Because some cards are open, every bet is made with partly known information. It shows how a game can let players read one another from what is shown, and how the open cards change who has to bet first.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 playing cards
- about 30 beans or coins for each player

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal two cards face down and then one face up to each player.
2. **First bet.** The player showing the highest card bets first. Others fold, call or raise in turn.
3. **Three more cards.** Deal one more card face up to each player still in, and bet again. Do this three times. Each time, the player whose open cards make the best hand bets first.
4. **Last card.** Deal one more card face down to each player and bet for the last time. Each player now has three cards hidden and four showing.
5. **Show.** Players still in turn their hidden cards up. Each makes the best five-card hand from their seven. The best hand takes the pot.
6. **Play on.** Play three hands. The player with the most chips wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did the open cards tell you about the other players' hands?
- When did you stay in with a weak hand, hoping for the next card?
- How would the game feel if all seven cards were hidden?

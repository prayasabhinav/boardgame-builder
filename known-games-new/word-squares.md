# Word Squares — everyone writes the same letters in their own grid

- From: Word Squares, a pencil and paper game
- Players: 2 or more players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Word Squares'

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player draws a grid of five by five squares. In turn, players call out a letter, and everyone writes that letter in a square of their own grid. After 25 letters the grids are full, and words across and down score.

## What it shows a designer

Everyone hears the same letters, yet everyone ends with a different grid. It shows how one shared sequence can give each player a private puzzle, and how choosing a letter to call helps you and the others differently.

## What you need

- paper and a pencil for each player
- a dictionary to settle doubts

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Each player draws a square of five by five boxes on their own paper.
2. **Call a letter.** The first player calls any letter of the alphabet. Everyone, the caller too, writes it in any empty box of their own grid. Once written, a letter cannot be moved.
3. **Take turns.** The turn to call goes round the group. Repeat until every grid has 25 letters.
4. **Find words.** Count words of three or more letters along rows and across columns. Words along the slanted lines do not count.
5. **Score.** A five-letter word scores 10, a four-letter word scores 5, and a three-letter word scores 1.
6. **Win.** The highest score wins.

## After playing, talk about

- What did you think about before you called a letter?
- Why did two players with the same letters end up with different scores?
- What would change if the letters were drawn from a bag?

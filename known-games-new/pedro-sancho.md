# Pedro Sancho — bid for trumps, and the Five is worth five

- From: Pedro Sancho, a card game that comes from Auction Pitch
- Players: Any number of players, six or eight make the best game
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 41
- This version: The book's Pedro variation. The Nine of trumps does not score, so a hand holds nine points, and each player starts on 21. For four players, deal six cards each. No penalties for playing the wrong suit (take the card back)

The dealer sells the right to name the trump suit to the highest bidder. The points in a hand are not equal: the Five of trumps, called Pedro, is worth five. Scores count down from 21, and a player who bids and fails is set back.

## What it shows a designer

Fixed prizes of different sizes sit inside every hand, and every player knows them before the deal. It shows how one big prize, here worth five of the nine points, makes a single card the centre of the whole hand, and how a player who cannot win can still decide who wins.

## What you need

- a pack of 52 playing cards, Ace high and Two low
- paper and a pen to keep the scores

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Give one card to each player, and the highest deals. The dealer gives six cards to each player, three at a time. No trump card is turned up. Each score starts at 21.
2. **Bid.** The dealer sells the right to name the trump suit. The player to the dealer's left bids first, and bids go round to the left. The round can repeat, with players raising, until nobody wants to bid higher. A bid is a number of points.
3. **Accept or lead.** The dealer takes the highest bid, or refuses it and leads the trump suit themselves. If nobody bids, the dealer leads. A dealer who refuses must make at least the highest bid offered, or be set back by it.
4. **Play tricks.** The buyer leads a card of the suit they want as trump. Everyone follows suit if they can. If not, they may play a trump or any card. The highest card of the suit led wins, but a trump beats other suits. After the first trick, the winner may lead any suit.
5. **Count the points.** High: one point for the highest trump played, to the player dealt it. Low: one point for the lowest trump played, to whoever wins it, and any trump can take it. Jack: one point, to whoever wins the Jack of trumps. Game: one point for the Ten of trumps, to whoever wins it. Pedro: five points for the Five of trumps, to whoever wins it. A trump higher than the card takes it.
6. **Settle.** A buyer who wins at least the points bid takes off those points and any extra. A buyer who falls short adds the bid to their own score and takes nothing off. Every other player takes off the points they won.
7. **Win.** The deal passes to the left. The first player whose score reaches nothing wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Who did you try to help or hold back when you played a card that held points?
- How high did you bid, and what in your hand told you?
- Why does Pedro change the game, when it is only one card?

# Yacht — roll three times, then fill one box

- From: Yacht, a dice game that Yahtzee grew from
- Players: 2–6 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; sold as Yahtzee from the 1950s
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Yacht'
- This version: Players keep a scorepad of twelve boxes, as the book describes, without the extra bonuses of Yahtzee

Each turn you roll five dice up to three times, keeping the dice you like and rolling the rest again. Then you must write your result in one of twelve boxes on your scorepad. Each box can be used only once.

## What it shows a designer

Because every box is used once, a bad throw has to go somewhere, and the choice of where is the game. It shows how re-rolls soften luck and how a scorepad turns that choice into a plan.

## What you need

- 5 dice
- paper and a pen for each player, with the twelve boxes written down the side

## How to play

1. **The pad.** Write these twelve boxes down the side of your paper: Aces, Deuces, Threes, Fours, Fives, Sixes, Full House, Four of a Kind, Little Straight, Big Straight, Yacht, Choice.
2. **Roll.** On your turn, roll all five dice. Keep any you like and roll the others again. You may do this a third time. You may stop earlier.
3. **Number boxes.** In Aces to Sixes, score the sum of the dice that show that number. Three fours score 12 in Fours.
4. **Other boxes.** Full House (three of one number and two of another) scores the sum of all five dice. Four of a Kind scores the sum of all five. Little Straight (1 to 5) or Big Straight (2 to 6) scores 30. Yacht (five of a kind) scores 50. Choice scores the sum of all five dice.
5. **Fill one box.** After your last roll, you must write your score in one box that is still empty, even if the score is zero.
6. **Win.** After twelve rounds, add up your boxes. The highest total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which box did you use to hide a bad throw?
- When did you keep going for a big box and give up a safe one?
- What did it feel like when only one box was left?

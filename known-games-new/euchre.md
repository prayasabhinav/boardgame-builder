# Euchre — name trump, win three of five

- From: Euchre, a partnership card game for four
- Players: 4 players, two teams of two
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 92–94
- This version: No playing alone, and the first side to 5 points wins

Four players sit in two teams, partners opposite each other. Each hand has just five tricks. Before play, someone decides what the trump suit will be. That side must then win at least three tricks, or the other side scores.

## What it shows a designer

Choosing the trump is a promise made for your whole team. The two Jacks of the trump colour become the strongest cards, which changes what every card is worth. And your partner may not tell you what they hold.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards with all the Twos to Sixes taken out (32 cards left)
- paper and a pen to keep score

## How to play

1. **Teams and deal.** Cut for partners: the two highest cards play against the two lowest. Deal 5 cards to each player, 2 then 3. Turn up the next card and leave it on the stock.
2. **Rank.** In an ordinary suit: Ace high, then King, Queen, Jack, Ten, Nine, Eight, Seven. In trumps, the Jack of the trump suit is highest (the right bower), the other Jack of the same colour is next (the left bower, and it counts as a trump), then Ace, King, Queen, Ten, Nine, Eight, Seven.
3. **Order up.** Starting left of the dealer, each player may say "I order it up" or pass. The dealer's partner may say "I assist". If someone orders it up, the turned-up suit is trumps, and the dealer swaps one card from their hand for the turned-up card.
4. **The dealer.** If all pass, the dealer may take up the card, or turn it down (face up under the stock).
5. **Name another suit.** If it is turned down, starting left of the dealer each player may name a different suit as trumps or pass. If all pass again, throw in the hands and the next player deals.
6. **Play.** The player left of the dealer leads. You must follow suit if you can. The highest card of the suit led wins, but a trump beats any other suit. The winner of a trick leads the next. Five tricks are played.
7. **Score.** If the side that chose trumps wins three or four tricks, it scores 1. All five tricks (a march) scores 2. If it wins fewer than three, it is euchred and the other side scores 2.
8. **No hints.** Partners may not tell each other what they hold. If they do, the other side scores 1.
9. **Game.** The first side to 5 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you decide whether to order up the trump, and how sure did you need to be?
- How did the left bower change which cards you thought were strong?
- How did you tell your partner something without saying it?

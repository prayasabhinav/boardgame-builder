# Enfle — tricks that count for nothing

- From: Enfle (Schwellen), a traditional card game
- Players: 4–6 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Enfle'
- This version: No stake. The first player out wins.

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player gets eight cards. Players follow the leader's suit and the highest card wins the trick. The trick has no value in itself. A player who cannot follow suit has to pick up every card in the trick. The first player to empty their hand wins.

## What it shows a designer

A trick-taking game turned upside down: winning a trick gives you nothing, and being unable to follow suit costs you. It shows how one changed rule reverses what the same mechanic means.

## What you need

- one deck of 52 playing cards, cut down to 8 cards for each player

## How to play

1. **Build the pack.** Take out cards until there are 8 for each player. Remove the same number from each suit, starting from the twos. Aces are high.
2. **Deal.** Deal every card out, so each player has 8.
3. **Lead.** The player left of the dealer leads any card.
4. **Follow.** In turn, each player plays a card of the suit led. If everyone follows, the highest card wins the trick. Put the trick aside; those cards are out of the game. The winner leads next.
5. **Cannot follow.** A player with no card of that suit picks up every card played to the trick so far and leads the next trick. They may lead the suit they could not follow.
6. **Go out.** The first player to play their last card wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which suit did you lead from, and why?
- When did you want to lose a trick, and when did you want to win it?
- How is this different from a trick game where tricks score points?

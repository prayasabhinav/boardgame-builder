# Quinze — fifteen, and not a point more

- From: Quinze, an old card game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 5–10 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 167
- This version: Going over fifteen loses the hand, as in Twenty-One, and the first to win five hands wins the game

Two players try to make a card total of fifteen, or as near as they can below it. It is Twenty-One cut shorter: one card each to begin, and each player decides how many more to take.

## What it shows a designer

With only two players and a low target, the game turns on guessing what the other person will do. It shows how a small change to a target number changes how risky each draw feels.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards
- paper and a pen to keep a tally of hands won

## How to play

1. **Card values.** An Ace counts one. Court cards count ten. Other cards count their number.
2. **Deal.** Cut for the deal; the lower card deals. The dealer gives one card to the other player and one to themselves.
3. **First player.** The other player looks at their card and either stands or takes more cards, one at a time, to get nearer fifteen.
4. **Dealer.** Then the dealer looks at their card and stands or draws in the same way.
5. **Show.** Both players turn their cards over. Whoever has fifteen, or the nearest total below it, wins the hand. Going over loses. Equal totals are a draw.
6. **Play on.** Swap the deal. The first player to win five hands wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Did the second player to draw have an advantage, and why?
- How is a target of fifteen different from twenty-one in how safe each draw feels?
- When did you guess what the other player held?

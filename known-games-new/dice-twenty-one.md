# Dice Twenty-One — stop before you pass twenty-one

- From: a one-die game of the Twenty-One kind
- Players: Several players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 183
- This version: Five rounds are played, and a round is worth one point

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player throws one die as many times as they like and adds up the spots. Reach twenty-one, or get as near as you can without passing it. Throw too many and you are out of the round.

## What it shows a designer

The whole game is one question asked again and again: one more throw, or stop. It shows how a game with no cards and almost no rules still makes players weigh a risk.

## What you need

- one die
- paper and a pen to keep running totals

## How to play

1. **Turn.** In turn, each player throws the die and writes the number down. They add it to their running total.
2. **Go on or stop.** After each throw, the player decides to throw again or stop. A player may keep going as many times as they like.
3. **Bust.** A total of twenty-two or more is a bust. That player is out of this round.
4. **Round winner.** When everyone has stopped or busted, the player with twenty-one, or the highest total under it, wins the round and scores one point. If totals tie, no one scores.
5. **Win.** Play five rounds. The player with the most points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- At what total did you stop, and did that change when you saw the others' totals?
- Did going second help you, and why?
- What would change if you could not see anyone else's total?

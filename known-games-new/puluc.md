# Puluc — carry your prisoners down the road

- From: Puluc, a game of the Kekchi people of Central America
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–25 min
- Made by: Traditional; played by the Kekchi people of Central America, who are descended from the Maya
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 89
- This version: Four coins stand for the four maize ears (heads is the yellow side), and one head scores 1, because the printed table is unclear for that throw

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players sit at the two ends of a short road of ten spaces and send five pieces each along it from their own end. Landing on an enemy piece takes it prisoner, and the prisoner is carried towards your end of the road. A prisoner that gets there is out of the game.

## What it shows a designer

A capture here is not final: a prisoner can be taken back, and a stack of prisoners can change hands again and again. It shows how a game can keep the outcome open by letting captures be undone.

## What you need

- ten small sticks or pencils laid in a row like the rungs of a ladder, or a strip of paper with ten boxes
- 5 counters of one kind and 5 of another kind
- 4 coins, where heads is the yellow side and tails is the black side

## How to play

1. **Sit at the ends.** Players sit at opposite ends of the ten spaces. Each player's pieces start off the road, and travel towards the far end.
2. **Throw.** Throw the four coins. Heads are yellow and tails are black. Two yellow score 2, three yellow score 3, four yellow score 5, four black score 4, and one yellow scores 1. Each player throws once to start, and the higher score goes first.
3. **Enter and move.** On your first throw, put one piece on the first space at your end. After that, a throw can either bring in another piece or move one piece forward by the score. You may not land on a space holding one of your own pieces.
4. **Take a prisoner.** If you land on a space with one enemy piece, put the enemy piece under yours. Now carry it with you towards the far end.
5. **Take a stack.** If you land on a stack of enemy pieces, put your piece on top. The whole stack turns round and travels towards your far end. A stack can take a single piece or another stack.
6. **Reach the end.** An exact throw is not needed. When a stack reaches the far end, the piece on top and every piece of its colour go back to their owner, ready to start again. The other colour's pieces are out of the game. A piece that gets there alone also goes back to its owner.
7. **Win.** You win when the other player has no pieces left.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you move a piece forward, and when did you bring in a new one instead?
- What happened when a stack with prisoners changed hands more than once?
- How did being able to lose a prisoner change how you played?

# Matadore — make seven

- From: Matadore, a domino game
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 189
- This version: The first player to reach 30 points wins, and the 28 pieces are made on paper slips. The book plays to 100

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players lay dominoes in a line, but instead of matching a number they must add up to seven. A five is answered by a two, a six by an ace. Four special pieces, the matadores, can be played anywhere.

## What it shows a designer

A one-line change to a matching rule gives a new game. It shows how tile placement can be turned on its head, and how a piece you cannot use, the blank, becomes a danger to hold.

## What you need

- a set of 28 dominoes, or 28 paper slips each drawn with two halves from blank to six, covering every pair once
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Draw.** Shuffle the pieces face down. Each player takes three pieces. Keep the last three pieces out of the game, so neither player knows exactly what the other holds. The rest form a stock.
2. **Lead.** The player with the highest double leads it. If neither has a double, the player with the highest piece leads.
3. **Make seven.** Each piece you play must touch an open end, and its half beside that end must add up to seven. A four needs a three, a six needs an ace.
4. **Matadores.** The double blank, six-one, five-two and four-three can be played anywhere at any time. Only a matadore can be played on a blank.
5. **Draw.** If you cannot play, draw from the stock until you can. When the stock is empty and you cannot play, pass. You must play if you can.
6. **Doubles.** A double plays as one half only: a double six needs an ace.
7. **End the hand.** The first player to play their last piece shouts "Domino" and scores the spots left in the other hand; a double left in hand counts twice its single. If both players are stuck, the player with fewer spots scores the other player's spots.
8. **Win.** Play more hands until one player reaches 30 points.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you hold back a matadore, and why?
- What happened when a blank end showed on the table?
- How did leaving three pieces out of the game change what you could guess about the other hand?

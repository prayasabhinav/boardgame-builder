# Snip, Snap, Snorem — pay when someone plays on your rank

- From: Snip, Snap, Snorem, a traditional card game (a descendant of a German game)
- Players: 4–8 players
- Time: 10–15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Snip/Snap/Snorum'
- This version: The author's optional ante rule is left out.

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

The deck is dealt out. One player leads a card, and the others may add cards of the same rank or pass. Each time someone adds a card on yours, you pay a counter. The first player out wins the pool.

## What it shows a designer

A game where playing a card can cost you, because the next player's good play makes you pay. It shows how a payment rule makes players want to be last in a run, not first.

## What you need

- one deck of 52 playing cards
- about 15 counters each, such as coins or buttons

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Deal every card out, one at a time. It does not matter if some hands have one more.
2. **Lead.** The player left of the dealer leads any card.
3. **Follow or pass.** Each player in turn plays a card of the same rank, or passes.
4. **Pay.** When two players in a row play (not pass), the first of the two pays. The leader pays 1 counter (snip). The second card pays 2 (snap). The third pays 3 (snorem). Say the word aloud when it happens.
5. **New lead.** The player who plays the fourth card of a rank leads any card to start again.
6. **Go out.** The first player to empty their hand wins. Each other player puts in one counter for every card left, and the winner takes the pool.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you pass even though you could play? Why?
- Who paid the most, and was that luck or the order of play?
- What would change if the payments went to the player after you?

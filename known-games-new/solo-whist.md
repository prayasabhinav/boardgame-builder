# Solo Whist — name your own task before play

- From: Solo Whist, a version of Whist
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 170
- This version: Four calls only, each ranked above the one before, and one point to the caller for success. The book lists more calls and does not give the scoring for them

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Four players are dealt thirteen cards each, and a trump suit comes from the last card. Before the first card is played, each player may call a task. The highest caller then tries to keep it, either with a partner or alone, against the others.

## What it shows a designer

Each player picks what winning means for this hand, and says it aloud. It shows how a goal chosen by the player changes a game with fixed rules, and how an odd goal, like losing every trick, turns the usual aim upside down.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** The lowest card cut deals the whole deck. The last card is turned up as trumps. Cards rank Ace high down to Two low.
2. **Calls, lowest to highest.** Starting left of the dealer, each player passes or makes a call that is higher than any made so far. The calls: Propose: ask a partner, to make eight of the thirteen tricks together. Solo: make five tricks alone. Misère: lose every trick. There are no trumps in this hand. Abondance: make nine tricks alone, naming any suit as trumps.
3. **Partner.** If the call is "propose", another player may accept. They then play on the caller's side against the other two.
4. **Play.** The player to the dealer's left leads. Everyone follows suit if they can. The highest card of the suit led wins, and trumps beat other suits, except in Misère. The winner of each trick leads next.
5. **Score.** The caller's side scores one point if it made its task, and each player on the other side scores one point if it did not. If everyone passes, deal again.

## After playing, talk about

- Which call did you make, and what in your hand told you to?
- How did hearing the call change what the other players did?
- What was it like to play for losing every trick?

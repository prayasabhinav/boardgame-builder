# Rethrow Race — a few chances to throw again

- From: Rethrow options, a rule change for any game that uses dice
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: R. Wayne Schmittberger, who suggests it for games where dice decide how far you move
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 229
- This version: A 50-square paper race; the book suggests four or five rethrows, and this uses four

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players race along a track by throwing two dice. Each player starts with a few rethrow coins. After seeing a throw, you may spend a coin to throw again.

## What it shows a designer

Dice make a game uncertain, but a rule can give players a way to answer a bad throw. It shows how a small, limited rescue turns a plain race into a game of deciding when a throw is bad enough to pay for.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with a track of 50 squares drawn in a line, numbered 0 to 50
- two dice
- two small counters
- four coins for each player, to be the rethrow coins

## How to play

1. **Set up.** Put both counters on square 0. Give each player four rethrow coins and keep them in view.
2. **Throw.** On your turn, throw both dice and add the two numbers.
3. **Rethrow.** If you do not like the throw, give one coin back and throw again. You must use the new throw. If you do not like that one either, you may spend another coin, and so on.
4. **Move.** Move your counter forward by the throw you keep.
5. **Run out.** When your coins are gone, you must keep your first throw each turn.
6. **Win.** The first counter to reach square 50 or beyond wins.
7. **Handicap (optional).** To make up for going first, give the second player an extra coin or two.

## After playing, talk about

- How bad did a throw have to be before you paid to throw again?
- Did you save coins for the end of the track or use them early? Why?
- What did giving one player more coins do to the game?

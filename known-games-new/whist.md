# Whist — win tricks with a partner you cannot talk to

- From: Whist, a card game that Bridge developed from
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in England and the United States
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Whist'
- This version: Four deals only, and the side with the higher total of points after the fourth deal wins

Four players sit in two partnerships, partners facing each other. Each deal, everyone plays thirteen cards one at a time, and the side that wins more than six tricks scores. There is no bidding and nobody's hand is shown.

## What it shows a designer

A trick is a small contest inside a bigger game. Because one suit is named trumps, a low card can beat a high one, so the same hand plays differently each deal. It also shows how partners can work together when talking about the cards is not allowed.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Pair up and deal.** Partners sit opposite each other. The dealer gives out the whole pack one card at a time, so each player has thirteen cards. The dealer turns the last card face up on the table.
2. **Name trumps.** The suit of that face-up card is trumps for this deal. Before the first trick, the dealer takes it back into the hand.
3. **Lead.** The player to the dealer's left plays any card. Going round clockwise, each player must play a card of the same suit if they have one. If not, they may play any card.
4. **Win the trick.** The highest trump in the trick wins it. If there is no trump, the highest card of the suit led wins. Cards rank from ace high down to two. The winner keeps the four cards face down in front of them and leads next.
5. **Score the deal.** After thirteen tricks, each side counts the tricks it won. A side with more than six scores one point for each trick over six. The other side scores nothing.
6. **Play four deals.** Pass the deal to the left each time. After the fourth deal, add up the points. The higher total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you tell your partner anything when you could not speak?
- When did a low trump beat a high card, and how did it change your plans?
- What would the game be like if the trump card stayed face up all deal?

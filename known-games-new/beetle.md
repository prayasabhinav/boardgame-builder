# Beetle — roll the die, draw the bug

- From: Beetle (sold as Cootie in 1948), a dice-and-pencil game
- Players: 2 to 6 players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 159

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player has paper and a pencil and tries to draw a whole beetle. A die decides which part you may add on each turn. Some parts have to come before others.

## What it shows a designer

A die that gives parts, not steps, makes a race where nobody can hurry another player. The order rule (body, then head, then the rest) shows how one small dependency can stop a player who rolls the wrong number.

## What you need

- a die
- a sheet of paper and a pencil for each player

## How to play

1. **Start.** Everyone rolls once. The highest number goes first. Then turns go round the table.
2. **Roll.** On your turn, roll the die once. The number tells you which part you may draw.
3. **Body first.** 1 is the body, 2 the head, 3 one leg, 4 one feeler, 5 one eye and 6 the tail. You cannot draw anything until you roll a 1 for the body.
4. **Head before feelers.** You may add feelers (4) or eyes (5) only after you have drawn the head (2). A roll you cannot use is lost, and your turn ends.
5. **Finish.** The first player to finish their beetle wins. The book does not list how many of each part make a complete beetle, so agree that before you start.

## After playing, talk about

- Which roll hurt most when you could not use it, and why?
- If the head rule were removed, how would the race change?
- Did you feel in control at any point, or was it all the die?

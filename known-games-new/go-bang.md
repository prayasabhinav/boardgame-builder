# Go-Bang — five in a row on a big grid

- From: Go-Bang (also called Gomoku), a paper and counter game from Japan
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 5–15 min
- Made by: Traditional; played in Japan, and brought to Europe about 1885
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 96
- This version: Played on squared paper with pens instead of 100 counters each, and a full grid ends the game as a draw

Two players take turns marking squares on a large grid. The first to mark five of their own squares in an unbroken line wins. It is like noughts and crosses on a much bigger board.

## What it shows a designer

The same winning pattern, a line, plays very differently when the board is large. It shows how board size changes a game from one that is soon solved into one that keeps surprising people.

## What you need

- squared paper with a grid of at least 18 by 18 squares
- two pens of different colours, or X and O

## How to play

1. **Draw the grid.** Use a grid of 18 by 18 squares, the size of the I-go board. Decide who is X and who is O.
2. **Mark.** Take turns writing your mark in any one empty square.
3. **Make five.** The first player with five of their own marks in an unbroken line wins. The line can run across, up and down, or diagonally.
4. **Draw.** If every square is marked and nobody has five in a row, the game is a draw.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you stop making your own line and start blocking the other player's?
- How is this game different from noughts and crosses on a small grid?
- What would change if the grid were only 9 by 9?

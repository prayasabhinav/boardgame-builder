# Lasers & Feelings — one number for a whole character

- From: Lasers & Feelings, a one-page role-playing game by John Harper
- Players: 3–5 players, one of whom runs the game
- Time: 30–60 min
- Made by: John Harper

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

The players are the crew of a spaceship. One player runs the game, describing what the crew meets and playing everyone else. Each character is a single number: low is good at Lasers, high is good at Feelings.

## What it shows a designer

One number on a character sheet is enough to make two different kinds of action matter. It shows how a role-playing game lets dice decide how a story turns while the players decide what their characters try.

## What you need

- six-sided dice, three for the table
- a sheet each to write your character
- a pencil each

## How to play

1. **Make a character.** Choose a style (such as alien, robot or savvy) and a role (such as pilot, doctor or soldier), give your character a name, and choose a number from 2 to 5.
2. **Set the scene.** The player running the game describes the threat the crew faces and where they are. The others say what their characters do.
3. **Roll.** When a character tries something risky, roll one die, plus one more if they are prepared and one more if they are an expert.
4. **Lasers or Feelings.** For Lasers (science, reason, calm action) each die under your number is a success. For Feelings (intuition, persuasion, passion) each die over your number is a success.
5. **Read the dice.** No success: it goes wrong, and the player running the game says how. One: you manage, with a complication. Two: you do it well. Three: you do it brilliantly, with an extra effect. A die exactly on your number lets you ask the player running the game a question, which they answer honestly.
6. **End.** The session ends when the crew has dealt with the threat, or failed to.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you choose Lasers over Feelings for the same problem?
- How did one number make your character feel like someone?
- Who decided how the story went: the dice, the players or the person running it?

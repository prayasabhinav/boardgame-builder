# Haggle — trade cards and secrets

- From: Haggle, a party game by Sid Sackson, in Robert Schmittberger's card version
- Players: 8 or more players, and the more the better
- Time: 20 min
- Made by: Sid Sackson; this card version is by Robert Schmittberger
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 142
- This version: The teacher or host writes the information sheets beforehand using the twelve example rules in the steps; a class can use fewer rules

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Every player is handed twelve playing cards and two secret slips that say how some cards score. Players trade cards and slips for a set time. Then each hands in a hand of cards and the host scores them.

## What it shows a designer

Nobody can see the whole scoring rule, so a slip is worth as much as a card. It shows how hidden rules and open bargaining together make players trade information, and how caps and bonuses keep a lucky deal from deciding the winner.

## What you need

- playing cards: three decks for 8 to 13 players, six decks for 14 to 26 players
- one envelope for each player
- the host's set of numbered rule slips, two copies of each, written before the game
- pencils and a clock

## How to play

1. **Prepare.** The host takes out cards so every suit has the same number, with twelve cards for each player. Each player's envelope holds twelve random cards and two different rule slips. Two copies of every slip exist.
2. **Write the slips.** Give each suit a basic value, such as hearts 4, clubs 3, spades 2, diamonds 1, spread over several slips so that two slips together say more than one alone. Add limits on the top cards, bonuses for low cards, and a few odd rules.
3. **Trade.** For the set time, trade cards for cards, slips for slips, or cards for slips.
4. **Hand in.** When time is up, put the cards you want to count into your envelope, with your name on it. More than 15 cards loses the extras at random.
5. **Score.** The host scores every envelope using the full set of slips. The highest total wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which was worth more to you, a card or a slip, and when did that change?
- Did anyone say something false while trading? What did it cost them?
- Why might the host cap the highest cards and give bonuses to low ones?

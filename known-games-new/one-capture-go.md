# One-Capture Go — the first surround wins

- From: Go, an old game from China, cut down to a single capture
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 10 min
- Made by: Traditional; the paper version is described by Robert Schmittberger
- Rules from: New Rules for Classic Games, p. 137
- This version: Played on a hand-drawn map of regions, with a different pencil each

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Two players take turns colouring in regions on a map. Nothing is ever taken off. The first player to colour the last open space around a group of the other player's regions wins at once.

## What it shows a designer

A game can end on its very first capture, so every move is both a threat and a risk. It also shows how a pie rule (one player sets up, the other chooses a side) can balance a game with a first-move advantage.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with an irregular map drawn on it: 20 to 30 regions of different shapes that touch along edges
- two pencils or pens of different colours

## How to play

1. **Draw the map.** Draw a map of regions on the paper. They can be any shapes. Regions that touch along an edge are neighbours; regions that touch only at a corner are not.
2. **Colour.** Take turns. On your turn colour in one region that is still empty, using your own colour. You may not pass.
3. **Surround.** A region, or a joined group of regions of one colour, is surrounded when every neighbour it has is coloured in.
4. **Win.** If your move surrounds a region or group of your opponent's, you win at once. If your move surrounds one of your own, you lose. If one move surrounds both players' regions, the player who moved wins.
5. **Balance it.** For a fairer game, use the pie rule: one player makes the first move for both colours, then the other player chooses which colour to play.

## After playing, talk about

- Which first moves felt safe, and which felt like walking into a trap?
- How did the shape of the map change where you wanted to play?
- Why might a pie rule make the first move less valuable?

# Botticelli — twenty questions with a trap

- From: Botticelli, a parlour guessing game
- Players: 3–8 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Big Book of Rules, p. 45
- This version: Played with famous people only, and the Thinker's answer is always checked by the group

One player, the Thinker, picks a famous person and gives the first letter of the surname. The others try to guess who by asking questions that point to other people with that letter. If the Thinker cannot name the person, the asker earns a question.

## What it shows a designer

A game can make the answerer's knowledge a test as well. It shows how asking about a different person, not the secret one, makes each question both a clue and a challenge to the Thinker.

## What you need

- nothing; a group of people who know a range of famous names

## How to play

1. **Choose.** The Thinker picks a famous person, living, dead or mythical, and says only the first letter, such as "I am B."
2. **Ask for another person.** A player asks a question about a person who starts with that letter, such as "Are you the actor who played the Godfather in the movies?"
3. **Thinker answers.** If the Thinker knows who the question describes, they answer "No, I'm not Marlon Brando." The question gets a name, not a yes.
4. **Stumped.** If the Thinker cannot name that person, the asker says who it was. The asker may then ask one question that can be answered yes or no.
5. **Try to name.** When a question seems to point to the real person, the Thinker may say "Yes, I am." The questioner then says the name. A wrong name leaves the game going and gives everyone a clue.
6. **Next Thinker.** The player who names the Thinker's person becomes the next Thinker.

## After playing, talk about

- Which of your questions were really about getting a clue, and which were traps?
- How did the group's different knowledge change what could be asked?
- When did the yes-or-no questions help most?

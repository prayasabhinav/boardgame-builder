# California Jack — a hand that refills from a face-up pile

- From: California Jack, a card game related to All-Fours
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, p. 40
- This version: Two players, and the game stops at 5 points instead of 10

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Each player holds six cards. The rest of the pack lies face up as a stock. After every trick, each player takes a card from the stock, so six cards stay in hand until the stock is gone. Points are won for the top trump, the bottom trump, the Jack of trumps and the card count.

## What it shows a designer

Most card games with tricks hide the cards still to come. Here they lie face up, so both players can see what is coming and can plan around it. It shows how opening up one piece of information changes what planning means.

## What you need

- a pack of 52 playing cards, Ace high and Two low
- paper and a pen to keep the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut the cards, and the highest card deals. The dealer gives each player six cards, three at a time.
2. **Stock and trump.** The dealer turns the rest of the pack face up as the stock. The top card shows the trump suit. The dealer then slips that card into the stock, near the middle. The stock stays face up.
3. **Lead.** The player who is not dealing leads any card. The other player plays a card to it. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you may play any card, not only a trump.
4. **Win the trick.** The highest card of the suit led wins, but a trump beats other suits. The winner leads next.
5. **Refill.** After each trick, the dealer gives each player one card from the stock, starting with the player who won the trick. Play goes on until the stock and the hands are all used.
6. **Score four points.** High: the Ace of trumps goes to whoever was dealt it, a sure point. Low: the Two of trumps goes to whoever wins it in a trick. Jack: the Jack of trumps goes to whoever wins it in a trick. Game: the player who won cards worth the most scores, Ace 4, King 3, Queen 2, Jack 1, Ten 10.
7. **Win.** The deal passes to the other player. The first to reach 5 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did seeing the stock change which cards you kept in your hand?
- Why is the Ace of trumps a sure point, and the Two not?
- What would you plan differently if the stock were face down?

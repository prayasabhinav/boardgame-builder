# Dara — place twelve, then make threes

- From: Dara, a game of the Dakarkari people of Nigeria
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; played by the Dakarkari people of Nigeria
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 95
- This version: Threes made while placing take nothing, and you lose if you are left with two pieces or cannot move

Made with boardgameBuilder 1.x. To design from it, open boardgameBuilder and choose Open game: its blocks go on the canvas, and the feelings are left for you to pick.

Players take turns putting twelve pieces each into a grid of 30 holes. Then they move one piece a step at a time. Three of your pieces in a straight row take one of the other player's pieces off the board.

## What it shows a designer

Where the pieces are placed in the first part decides the second part. It shows how a game can start with a quiet building phase and turn into a sharp fight over single moves.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with 30 dots or small circles in 5 rows of 6
- 12 coins of one kind and 12 buttons of another kind

## How to play

1. **Draw the holes.** Draw 5 rows of 6 dots. Pieces sit on the dots.
2. **Place.** Take turns putting one piece on any empty dot until each player has put down all twelve. Six dots stay empty. No piece is taken during this part, even if you make a row of three.
3. **Move.** Now take turns moving one of your pieces one step, up, down, left or right, onto an empty dot. Diagonal steps are not allowed.
4. **Make a three.** If a move puts three of your pieces in a straight row, side by side, up and down or across, take any one of the other player's pieces off the board. A row of four does not count.
5. **Win.** You win when the other player is down to two pieces, or has no move.

## After playing, talk about

- Where did you place your pieces first, and why there?
- What did the six empty dots do to the moving part of the game?
- Why does a row of four not count, and how does that change what you aim for?

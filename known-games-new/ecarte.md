# Ecarte — swap cards, win three of five tricks

- From: Ecarte, a card game for two
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15 min
- Made by: Traditional
- Rules from: The Everything Games Book, pp. 83–84
- This version: First to 5 points, with the table of recommended hands left out

Two players get five cards each. Before play, the player who goes first may ask to swap cards, and the dealer can agree or refuse. Then they play five tricks. Winning three tricks scores a point, and all five scores two.

## What it shows a designer

The swap is a bet on your own hand, and it is made out loud. Refusing or playing on without asking carries a risk: if you fail, the other player scores double. The game is full of small decisions about whether to trust the cards you have.

## What you need

- a deck of 52 cards with all the Twos to Sixes taken out (32 cards left)
- paper and a pen to keep score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Cut for the deal; the highest card deals. Deal 5 cards to each player, 2 at a time and then 3 at a time. Turn up the next card: its suit is trumps. If it is a King, the dealer scores 1. Put the rest face down as the stock.
2. **Rank.** From highest to lowest: King, Queen, Jack, Ace, Ten, Nine, Eight, Seven.
3. **Propose.** The non-dealer may say "Cards" to ask for a swap, or just start playing. If they ask, the dealer may say "I accept" or "I refuse".
4. **Swap.** If the dealer accepts, the non-dealer puts some cards face down and takes the same number from the stock. The dealer may swap too. The non-dealer may ask again, and the dealer may accept or refuse again.
5. **The King.** A player holding the King of trumps says so before playing a card, and scores 1.
6. **Play.** The non-dealer leads one card. The other player must follow suit if they can, and must win the trick if they can. With no card of that suit, they must play a trump if they have one. The winner of each trick leads the next.
7. **Score.** Win three or four tricks and score 1. Win all five and score 2.
8. **The penalty.** If the non-dealer played without asking, or the dealer refused the first request, and that player does not win three tricks, the other player scores 2.
9. **Game.** The first to 5 points wins.

## After playing, talk about

- When did you ask for cards, and when did you play with what you had?
- What did the other player's refusal tell you about their hand?
- Why does the game charge a penalty for refusing or standing pat?

# Spades — say how many tricks you will take, and take exactly that many

- From: Spades, an American card game of the Whist family
- Players: 4 players
- Time: 20–30 min
- Made by: Traditional; devised in the United States in the 1930s and spread by soldiers in the Second World War
- Rules from: Hoyle's Rules of Games, under 'Spades'
- This version: Four deals, with Nil as the only special bid, and the higher total after the fourth deal wins

Partners add their bids together into one promise. Spades are always trumps. Each deal, a side wins points for keeping its promise and loses them if it falls short.

## What it shows a designer

A bid turns a hand of cards into a target. It shows how asking players to name their goal first makes everyone judge their own cards before the play begins, and how a bold special bid like Nil adds a risk that only one player carries for the side.

## What you need

- one pack of 52 playing cards
- paper and a pen for the score

## How to play

1. **Deal.** Partners sit opposite each other. The dealer gives each player thirteen cards. Spades are trumps, and ace is the highest card of a suit.
2. **Bid.** Starting with the dealer, each player says how many tricks they expect to win, from zero to thirteen. A player may bid Nil instead, meaning they will win no tricks. Partners' bids are added together. That total is their side's contract.
3. **Lead and follow.** The player to the dealer's left leads any card except a spade. Spades may be led only after a spade has been played on an earlier trick, or if the leader has nothing else. Everyone must follow suit if they can.
4. **Win the trick.** The highest spade wins a trick, or else the highest card of the suit led. The winner keeps the trick and leads next.
5. **Score the deal.** If a side wins at least as many tricks as its contract, it scores 10 points for each trick bid and 1 point for each extra trick. If it wins fewer, it loses 10 points for each trick bid. A player who bid Nil adds 100 points for taking no tricks and loses 100 if they take any. The Nil player's partner is scored on their own bid.
6. **Play four deals.** Pass the deal to the left. The side with the higher total after four deals wins.

## After playing, talk about

- How did you decide your bid, and how did your partner's bid change it?
- When did you take a trick you did not want, and what could you have done?
- Was a Nil bid worth the risk when you made it?

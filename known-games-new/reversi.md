# Reversi — turn their pieces to yours

- From: Reversi, a board game from the 1880s
- Players: 2 players
- Time: 15–20 min
- Made by: Traditional; invented between 1880 and 1890
- Rules from: Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, p. 74
- This version: A player who cannot place a piece passes, and 64 coins (heads for Black, tails for White) stand for the two-coloured pieces

Two players place pieces on an 8 by 8 board. A piece that is placed can trap a straight line of the other player's pieces and turn them over to its own colour. When the board is full, the colour showing on more squares wins.

## What it shows a designer

A captured piece does not leave the game; it changes side. So a big lead can swing back in a single move, and the count means little until the last square is filled.

## What you need

- a sheet of paper with an 8 by 8 grid of squares (64 squares)
- 64 coins, where heads is Black and tails is White (or paper discs coloured on each side)

## How to play

1. **Fill the middle.** Black puts a coin on one of the four middle squares, heads up. White puts a coin on another of the four, tails up. Take turns until all four middle squares are filled.
2. **Place.** From then on, put a coin with your colour up on an empty square that touches at least one of the other player's coins.
3. **Turn pieces over.** Look along the row, the column and both diagonals from the coin you just placed. If a straight line of the other player's coins lies between it and another coin of yours, turn every coin in that line over to your colour.
4. **Keep going.** A coin can change colour many times in one game. If you cannot place a coin, pass.
5. **Count.** When all 64 squares are covered, count each colour. The player with more coins showing wins.

## After playing, talk about

- Which squares did you take early, and what did they let the other player do?
- When did one move turn over a long line, and could you have seen it coming?
- How did a big lead in the middle of the game change by the end?

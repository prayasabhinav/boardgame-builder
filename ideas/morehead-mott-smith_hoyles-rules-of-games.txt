idea: A payment that grows with the card shown | In Beggar-Your-Neighbor an ace demands four cards, a king three, a queen two and a jack one. If a face card comes up during the payment, the duty reverses. The turn of a single card passes a debt back and forth. | under 'Beggar-Your-Neighbor'
idea: A reflex game needs rules about hands | In Slapjack the rules say cards are turned away from the owner, turning and slapping use the same hand, the lowest hand nearest the jack wins when many slap, and a wrong slap costs a card. The book adds that these are to be enforced to avoid mayhem. | under 'Slapjack'
idea: A player who has lost everything can still return | In Slapjack a player with no cards stays in until the next jack. If he slaps it first he continues, otherwise he is out. | under 'Slapjack'
idea: A parlour game uses almost nothing | The book says most parlour games need pencils and paper, sometimes not even that. Some are real games, some are only stunts, and some are intellectual contests that interest keen competitors. | under 'Parlor Games'
idea: A hidden word can be reached by rhyme and mime | Dumb Crambo has one player give a rhyming clue, and the others mime words that rhyme, which are not spoken. Players guess the mime and so reach the secret word. | under 'Dumb Crambo'
idea: A hide and seek with no winner | In Hot Buttered Beans the players help the seeker by calling cold, warm, hot or burning. The book says there are no winners or losers and each player takes a turn at seeking. | under 'Hot Buttered Beans'
idea: Hidden in plain sight | In Hunt the Thimble the object sits in view in a cluttered room. A seeker who sees it sits down silently and does not stare, so the others cannot tell. The last seeker is the target of gentle jibes. | under 'Hunt the Thimble'
idea: Free-for-all play needs calling and challenge rules | Anagrams has no turns for spotting words, so the book lists rules for who calls first, what a longer word does against a shorter one, what counts as a foul, and how a challenge is settled by a dictionary chosen in advance. | under 'Anagrams'
idea: A shared stock of letters can be taken from other players | In Anagrams a player may use a tile in the middle and a word before another player to make a new word. Owners can add to their own words to make them harder to steal. | under 'Anagrams'
idea: A game with no equipment but the world | In Alphabet the players find each letter from A to Z on signs and number plates, and may play alone, in competition or co-operatively. The book lists safety rules for drivers and a rule about graveyards that sends players back to A. | under 'Alphabet'
idea: A bluff in a word game | In Ghosts, players add letters in turn and each hopes not to complete a word. A player may challenge the one before him to name a word starting with the letters so far. The book says it is often necessary to bluff by calling a letter with no word in mind. | under 'Ghosts'
idea: Losses spell out a word | A player who loses a round in Ghosts takes the next letter of GHOSTS, and with all six he is out. The loss counter is also a word, and the last one left wins. | under 'Ghosts'
idea: A category chain | In Geography each player names a place that begins with the last letter of the previous place. The same format works for any category, and the book says to define it carefully at the start to avoid arguments. | under 'Geography'
idea: A solved game becomes a children's game | The book says players soon find that correct play by both sides in Tic-Tac-Toe leads to a draw, so the game is mostly played by young children who have not yet found how to force a draw. | under 'Tic-Tac-Toe'
idea: Every player fills the same grid with the same calls | In Word Squares and Poker Squares each person writes one called letter or card into a 5 by 5 square, never to be moved, and scores rows and columns after twenty-five calls. The calls are the same for everyone, so the difference is where each person puts them. | under 'Word Squares'
idea: Scoring by what others did not write | In Guggenheim each space scores 1 point for every other player who did not write the same word. The more common an answer, the less it earns. | under 'Guggenheim'
idea: Hidden ships, shared guesses | In Battleship each side hides ships on a grid and fires a salvo at squares. The opponent reports how many hits on which ship without saying which shots hit. A sunk ship reduces the owner's next salvo by the number of shots it represented. | under 'Battleship'
idea: Yes or no questions halve the possibilities | The book says that if every question in Twenty Questions removes half the subjects, twenty questions can tell apart 1,048,576 things. That works only if each question is chosen to split what remains in half. | under 'Twenty Questions'
idea: Naming a guess every turn keeps pressure on | In Twenty Questions the questioner has to make a specific guess after each question. The clue of animal, vegetable or mineral can give odd results, such as a wooden table counted as a vegetable. | under 'Twenty Questions'
idea: A refinement where the answerer has to produce names | In Botticelli the questioner asks something that has a name as answer, and It has to supply a name beginning with the announced letter. If It cannot, the questioner may ask a direct yes or no question. | under 'Botticelli'

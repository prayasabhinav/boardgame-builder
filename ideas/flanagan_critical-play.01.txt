idea: A game changes when people leave it | Brand's Theory of Game Change says a game is not altered by winning, judging or watching it, but by leaving it and starting a new game elsewhere. If the new one works, it may alter or replace the old one. | p. 184
idea: The mechanic is the message | Flanagan holds that knowledge in games comes through doing, so what a player does with the game's verbs matters to the meaning. She writes that a game's mechanic is its message. | p. 185
idea: Play makes nothing | Caillois says play creates no wealth or goods, so that property is exchanged but nothing is produced and nothing is harvested. Flanagan finds conceptual art close to this, because it favours the system and process over a product. | p. 186
idea: Critical games often lack a clear winner | Salen and Zimmerman and Crawford would say a game needs at least a winner or loser, and many artists' games are abstract or open. Flanagan points out that automatic writing, Fluxus action and a finished exquisite corpse can each be read as a quantifiable outcome. | p. 186

# Components for keying "needs review"

Written on 6 November 2026 on his word ("Board game components for needs review kind of use case needs a review of all ways which are ok to understand a structure of what is not. You do it."), after reading every pair a known game shows: 584 pairs, 514 of them outside Theme. What was found is in `fine-pairs-review.md`. The definitions below are a proposal of mine until he changes or keeps them. The same day he chose this read to key the first sheet in his place ("Use the read of the patterns that emerges - not possible fore to key"); those keys are marked "pattern read" in keys.jsonl, and a key of his on a pair outranks them. It holds for this sheet and this model only, not as a rule (his words: "It is not a rule, ot holds for this case, this model"). Under each pair on a sheet, the lines 1:, 2: and 3: take what each component is for that pair.

1. name: the thing
   what it is: what the core mechanic puts in a player's hands or on the table, named as an object or an act: a card played to a trick, a piece on a track, a throw of the dice, seeds sown into a pit, a word said aloud.
2. name: the partner's question
   what it is: the partner block's slot asked of that thing, with the partner's answer. Victory condition: how does the thing count towards winning or losing? Player interaction: whom does it reach? Chance or skill: does luck or choice decide it? Information visibility: who can see it? Timing: when is it done, and who waits? Simulation: what in the world does it copy? Theme: what does it stand for?
3. name: what happens at the table
   what it is: the move, choice or event players make because the answer in 2 holds for the thing in 1: a trump played onto a rival's high card, the heaviest cards handed on in a pass, a race to empty the hand.

Which key follows from them (r needs review, f fine, ? don't know):

f when 1 names a thing, 2 answers its question about that same thing, and 3 names a move players make because of it.

r when any one of these holds. 2 cannot be asked of the thing in 1, because the partner needs something the core mechanic does not make (a hidden hand, where the thing is seeds in open pits). The core mechanic already answers 2's question another way, so the pair gives two answers to one question (chance alone decides, asked whether luck or choice decides; hide and find, asked who can see it, with everything face up). 3 is empty, because the partner changes nothing at the table (a hidden hand, where every card is played in a fixed order).

? when 1 cannot be named, because how the core mechanic is played is not known.

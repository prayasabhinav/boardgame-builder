# What the fine pairs share, and what a pair needing review lacks

Written 6 November 2026. I read every core-mechanic pair that a known game shows (584, from games.json and known-games-new/*.md; 514 outside Theme), each with the entry the tool shows for it, and the six conflicts the knowledge base already holds. The components in `components.md` come from this reading.

## What every fine pair does

In each of the 514 entries, the partner block takes hold of one concrete thing the core mechanic makes, and says what happens to it. Trick taking and fewest points: tricks carry points against you, so players slip low cards under the winner. Make a row and take a piece with direct confrontation: a row is an attack. Calling a contract with hidden hands: a call is made on a hand nobody else can see. The thing is always named (a card, a piece, a throw, a whisper, a pile), and the entry always ends in something players do.

Each slot asks the thing one question, and the entries answer it the same way across slots. Victory condition: the win is counted in the core mechanic's own unit (each set laid scores; each trick won moves your piece forward). Player interaction: the thing is aimed at a rival, lands on something shared, or stays on one's own board. Chance or skill: the entry says where luck enters the thing (the deal, the throw, the draw) and where choice enters. Information visibility: the entry says which of the core mechanic's things are seen, and by whom. Timing: the entry says when the thing is done and who watches meanwhile. Simulation and Theme: what the thing copies or stands for.

## What a pair needing review lacks

The six conflicts in the knowledge base (solo in parallel with direct confrontation, fully cooperative with last standing or direct confrontation, mostly skill with mostly chance, full information with hidden hands, model a system with abstract) all have one shape: two blocks answer the same question differently. Read against the 514, three ways of failing show. The partner asks its question of something the core mechanic does not make. The core mechanic has already answered the partner's question, so the pair answers it twice. Or the partner changes nothing at the table.

## Known-game pairs that fail the components

Five pairs a known game shows fail them, and their own entries say so. Chance alone decides with hidden hands ("holding it hidden protects nothing"), with full information, with everyone at once ("the reveal is only a read-out") and with mostly chance ("both blocks say the same thing"); hide and find with full information ("there is nothing left to hide"). `labels.py` marks every known-game pair fine, so these five train as fine unless he keys them. None of them is on a sheet, since sheets take only pairs no known game shows.

## What the reading cannot reach

18 core mechanics appear in no known-game template: betting on a throw, build your own side, catch-up help, chase and tag, connection, counter cycle, different powers, doubling the stakes, forced capture, grow stronger, one-use powers, promotion, relay, safe shape, sowing seeds, spend to use, two games at once, wraparound board. The templates are card, word and party games; the board games these come from (Bell, Schmittberger) have no templates. Every pair of theirs on a sheet is keyed without a fine example behind it.

27 partner blocks appear in no known game either, among them traitor, semi-cooperative, cooperative survival, hidden personal goal, finish on exact number and dummy hand.

## Seen in passing

Some fine-pair entries do not show the meeting: area control with mostly skill (a rule about repeated positions), bidding with mostly skill, area control with take turns. A few carry judging words the drafting brief avoids (fun, exciting, hilarious, pretty), all in older curated entries. Pattern building with race to finish says "his early moves" of a player. None of these were changed.

# Sources for the newer blocks

Written 16 October 2026, 13:47. The 31 blocks added on 16 October 2026 were each drawn from a game, a practice or a piece of writing. This file names them, so a reader can follow a block back to where it came from. Each source with a DOI was found through Consensus, checked to resolve and to carry no retraction through find-research-papers, and settled against its Crossref record for authors and dates. Sources with no registry record are listed separately at the end, as found but not confirmed.

A source here says where an idea came from. It is not a claim that the source endorses the block, and the blocks' feedback names no games, since none of those entries has been checked against BoardGameGeek.

## Seventeen unusual blocks

- **call and response**: poetry games such as *Crow*, and the language play in Flanagan (2009).
- **playing in silence**: silent cooperative card games such as *The Mind*.
- **change the rules**: Peter Suber's *Nomic*, and Balcı, Iacovides and Kirman (2026) on designing for rule-changing play.
- **no objective**: games that end without a winner, such as Amabel Holland's *Endurance*; Legaard (2023) on wonder as play without an end point.
- **set your own goal**: *Endurance*, where players decide afterwards what counted as doing well.
- **one loser**: games that end when one player loses, such as *Cockroach Poker*.
- **playing by letter**: correspondence play, such as *Missives of Mystery*, played over a calendar year.
- **absent player**: board games buried with the dead in ancient Egypt, and the ghost who can only hint in *Mysterium*.
- **chance read as a sign**: dice divination in India, Tibet and China (Dotson, Cook and Lu, 2021).
- **attunement**: *The Mind*.
- **poetic**: game poems (Almo and Magnuson, 2026) and poetic gameplay (Mitchell, 2016).
- **ritual or sacred**: gyan chaupar, the source of Snakes and Ladders (Topsfield, 2021; Frembgen, 2024), and the Buddhist game *Selection of Buddhas* (McGuire, 2014).
- **mortality and loss**: *Passage* (Mengi, 2026).
- **rules you discover**: card games such as *Mao*, where newcomers learn the rules by breaking them.
- **one knows, cannot say**: *Mysterium*.
- **irreversible change**: legacy board games (Katsantonis, 2025).
- **real-time span**: *The Longing*, a game that runs on real time (Ensslin, Aksay and Richter, 2026). Moved to the Timing group on 17 October 2026.

## Fourteen blocks from Dhruv Jani's ideas

- **role-play**: tabletop role-playing games (Katifori et al., 2022).
- **dexterity**: dexterity games such as Jenga and carrom.
- **drawing the map**: *The Quiet Year*, where a community's map is drawn as it is played.
- **keep the game going**: James Carse's infinite games, played to keep play going.
- **cannot be won**: games that refuse to be played, such as Zach Gage's *Lose/Lose*.
- **game master**: the game master as co-author of a tabletop role-playing game (Katifori et al., 2022) and the power that role holds (Bowman, 2013).
- **public game**: street games, as recorded in Douglas (1916).
- **dice tell the story**: dice and narrative in *Dungeons & Dragons* (Salthouse, 2025).
- **war and conquest**: chess and its origin in chaturanga; board wargames that argue about a conflict (Ambrosio and Ross, 2022).
- **map revealed as you go**: no outside source found; from the curriculum's *"the pleasure of the unrevealed grid"*.
- **rules make an argument**: procedural rhetoric (Bogost, 2007), and evidence that players do read arguments in rules (Anderson, Karzmark and Wardrip-Fruin, 2019).
- **stakes outside the game**: the magic circle and its leaks into everyday life (LeClaire, 2020; Bowman, 2013).
- **points stand for values**: value capture (Nguyen, 2024) and the critique of gamification (Ferrara, 2013).
- **rehearse a future**: role-play as a rehearsal for other ways of living (Kawitzky, 2020).

## Timing, added 17 October 2026

The Timing group gathers when and how fast players act. I asked whether pace was a missing category, chose the name Timing, and added *sedated* and *accelerated*; *real-time span* moved here from Simulation. The April notes on feelings had already named a block called *timers*, which this group makes real as *timed turns*.

- **take turns**: the turn order of most board games.
- **everyone at once**: simultaneous action selection, as BoardGameGeek calls it; *7 Wonders*, where every player picks a card at the same moment.
- **no turns**: real-time play, as BoardGameGeek calls it; *Set*.
- **timed turns**: *3 Quarters* (Prayas Abhinav, 2022), where every move has 20 seconds.
- **sedated**: my word for play slowed down on purpose.
- **accelerated**: my word for play that speeds up as it goes; *Tetris*, where pieces fall faster at every level.

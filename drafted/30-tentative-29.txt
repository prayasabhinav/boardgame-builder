The quickest player gets the first move. | The pick is made before the rush. | The setter has little time to adjust. | Speed can overrule the setup. | A fast hand can win before the pick matters.
pie-rule+timed-turns | One player sets up with a clock running, and the other picks a side in the same time. Each has seconds to judge the start. | Both players have to choose fast. | The setup shows in a glance. | Judging an even start is hard in seconds. | A rushed pick can be wrong.
pie-rule+real-time-span | One player sets the game on day one and the other picks a side the next morning. Play then goes on over days. | The pick has time. | The setter waits for the answer. | A long wait before the first move. | A forgotten pick can stall the game.
pie-rule+sedated | One player sets up slowly, with a breath at each step, and the other takes time before picking a side. The slowness gives the choice more care. | The pick is made slowly. | The chooser has time to look. | The start takes long. | The calm can feel too slow.
pie-rule+accelerated | One player sets up a game that speeds up as it goes, and the other picks a side. A side that starts slowly may have more time to catch up. | The setter thinks about the rush. | The chooser picks with speed in mind. | Speed is hard to weigh in advance. | The final rush can undo the pick.
pie-rule+extra-turn | One player sets the start, including where extra turns sit, and the other picks a side. pie-rule+staged-game | One player sets up the first stage, and the other picks a side for it. What the setter does in stage one shapes the second, so they have two things to even out. | The setter plans both stages at once. | The chooser looks ahead to stage two. | Two stages are hard to balance. | Stage one choices last into stage two.
pie-rule+model-a-system | One player sets the starting state of the system, such as how much water each field has, and the other picks which group to play. The setter shapes the start so that no group is plainly ahead. | The setter checks how much each group starts with. | The chooser picks a group. | A system is hard to even out. | A small difference can grow over the rounds.
pie-rule+stakeholder-roles | One player writes the needs of each group, and the other picks which group to play. A setter who makes the farmers' needs very heavy knows the chooser may take the council. | The setter weighs one group's needs against another's. | The chooser takes a viewpoint. | Groups are hard to match. | A group with few needs can look idle.
pie-rule+shared-resource | One player sets how much of the shared resource there is and who holds what, and the other picks a side. A setter who gives one side a bigger share knows the other will be chosen. | The setter checks who holds what. | The chooser picks a share. | Shares are hard to even out. | A shared resource can run out before the pick shows its effect.
pie-rule+delayed-effects | One player sets the start, and the other picks a side. Since choices show their effects only a few rounds later, neither can tell early which side is ahead. | The setter cannot rig an effect nobody can see yet. | The pick is made without knowing the effect. | Effects are hard to see in advance. | The result may not show until late.
pie-rule+try-a-rule | The pie rule is itself a rule the group can try. Players use it for one game and see what it changes about how the setter behaves. | The group can try it on the next game. | Players can play once with it and once without. | One game may not show much. | The effect on the setter can be hard to see.
pie-rule+debrief | After the game, players talk about the setup and the pick. The setter says what they were aiming for and the chooser says what they saw. | The talk shows both viewpoints. | The setter learns how a start is read. | Details of the setup are easy to forget. | Some players may not want to say.
pie-rule+irreversible-change | One player sets up a game where moves cannot be undone, and the other picks a side. The pick stays with the player for ever. | The pick is lasting. | The setter knows the setup will last. | There is no second try. | A wrong pick shows up in every later game.
pie-rule+rules-make-an-argument | The pie rule says that whoever sets a start can expect it to be judged by the other. A game built on it makes the point that you cannot keep the whole advantage. | The rule makes a point about sharing. | Players learn it by setting and choosing. | Players may never notice the point. | The rule may tell players what they do not want to hear.
pie-rule+stakes-outside-the-game | One player sets the terms of a bet over real money or a promise, and the other picks a side. pie-rule+points-stand-for-values | One player decides how many points each value is worth, and the other picks which side to score for. pie-rule+rehearse-a-future | One player sketches how the shared future will start, and the other picks a role in it.

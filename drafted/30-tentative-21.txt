growing-list+model-a-system | Items on the list are parts of a system, such as wells or fields, and the list tracks changes to them as play goes on. | Players learn the parts of the system. | Causes are visible. | A real system has more parts than a list can hold. | Parts of the system drop off the list.
growing-list+stakeholder-roles | Each player adds items from the point of view of their group, such as farmers or the village council. The list ends up holding many interests. | Players hear other views. | Items carry needs. | One group's items can crowd out the others. | Players favour their own group.
growing-list+shared-resource | Adding an item draws something from a shared supply, and the list records each draw. The total creeps towards the limit. | Players see the supply shrink. | The draws are recorded. | Over-drawing is easy to do. | Arguments start over who took the most.
growing-list+delayed-effects | An item added now has its effect a few rounds later, so players have to remember what they put on the list earlier. | Players link past items to results. | The list explains what happened. | Effects land long after the cause. | Forgotten items produce surprises.
growing-list+try-a-rule | The group tries a new rule about the list, such as shortening it, then plays on to see what changes. | The rule gets tested. | Players compare before and after. | A rule can break the list. | Resetting takes time.
growing-list+debrief | After the game the list is read out and the group talks about which parts were easy or hard to remember and why. | Players learn about their own memory. | The list is a record to talk over. | Players may disagree about what they remembered. | Some players do not want to talk.
growing-list+irreversible-change | Items are written in ink or stuck to the board, and the list from this game starts the next one. | Lists carry over. | Nothing can be undone. | Errors stay. | Early items shape later games.
growing-list+rules-make-an-argument | A list that only grows and never shrinks says something about how things pile up. The rules make that point by what they allow. | Players notice what the list favours. | It starts a talk. | The meaning differs from player to player. | Players may not notice the point at all.
growing-list+stakes-outside-the-game | A real promise or a small bet rides on getting the list right, so a slip costs something outside the game. | Players concentrate. | The result matters beyond the table. | A slip can cause upset. | Friends feel awkward.
growing-list+points-stand-for-values | Each item is worth points for something like kindness or health, and players start choosing items for the points and forget what the items were about. | Players see a number take the place of what it stood for. | Players discuss the choice. | Items lose their meaning. | Chasing points takes over.
growing-list+rehearse-a-future | The items on the list are things a future village does each day, and players add to it to see how a day would feel. | A future is spelled out item by item. | Players test it in words. | Items can drift from what is realistic. | A day is hard to repeat the same way.
